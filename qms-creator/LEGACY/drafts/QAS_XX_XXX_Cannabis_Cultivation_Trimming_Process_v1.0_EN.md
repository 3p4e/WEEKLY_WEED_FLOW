### 4. PROCEDURE

#### 4.1 Prerequisites

Before commencing the Cannabis Cultivation Trimming Process, personnel must ensure the fulfillment of the following prerequisites:

1. **Training:** All personnel involved in the trimming process must have completed the General GMP training module, which lasts approximately 1 hour. This training provides a foundation in good manufacturing practices, crucial for maintaining quality and compliance during the trimming process.

2. **Equipment:** Ensure that all required equipment for the trimming process is available and in good working condition. This includes, but is not limited to, trimming scissors, gloves, and appropriate containers for both trimmed and untrimmed material.

3. **Safety:** Personnel must wear the required personal protective equipment (PPE) throughout the trimming process. This typically includes gloves, masks, and protective eyewear to prevent any contact with the plant material and to maintain a hygienic environment.

#### 4.2 Process Steps

1. **Preparation:** 
   - Ensure that the workspace is clean and sanitized.
   - Prepare all required equipment and PPE before starting the trimming process.
   
2. **Inspection:** 
   - Examine the cannabis plants to be trimmed, ensuring they are dry and free from any mold or pests.
   
3. **Trimming:** 
   - Begin by removing the large fan leaves using your hands or scissors. These leaves do not contain trichomes and are not typically used in cannabis products.
   - Next, using the trimming scissors, carefully trim away the smaller sugar leaves that are close to the buds. These leaves have trichomes but are less potent than the buds themselves.
   - Aim to give the buds a uniform shape, removing any leaf material that protrudes from the bud structure.

4. **Sorting:** 
   - Separate the trimmed buds based on size, quality, and intended use (e.g., flowers, extracts).

5. **Cleaning:** 
   - Clean and sanitize all equipment and the workspace after the trimming process is complete to prevent contamination.

#### 4.3 In-Process Controls

- **Quality Check:** At various stages of the trimming process, particularly after the initial fan leaf removal and final bud trimming, inspect the product to ensure that the trimming is consistent and meets the specified quality criteria.
- **Hygiene Monitoring:** Regularly check that all personnel adhere to the required hygiene practices, including the use of PPE and proper handling of the plant material.
- **Equipment Check:** Before and after the trimming process, ensure that all equipment is functioning correctly and is properly sanitized to maintain product quality and safety.

#### 4.4 Completion Criteria

The Cannabis Cultivation Trimming Process is considered complete when:

- All specified cannabis plants have been trimmed according to the outlined quality and safety standards.
- The trimmed buds are sorted and ready for further processing or packaging.
- The workspace and all equipment used in the process have been cleaned and sanitized.
- A final inspection confirms that the trimmed product meets the required quality specifications and that all process documentation is complete and accurate.

Compliance with these completion criteria ensures the production of high-quality, safe cannabis products, maintaining the integrity of the cultivation process and adhering to regulatory standards.

7. DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

The Documentation Requirements section establishes the necessary documents that must be maintained to ensure compliance, traceability, and quality assurance throughout our processes. This includes the creation, review, approval, and retention of critical documents. Below are the specific requirements for Batch Records, Logs, and the Retention Policy.

7.1 Batch Records

7.1.1 Creation: Batch Records must be generated for each production batch. These records should include detailed information on the batch ingredients, quantities, batch number, production date, and any deviations from the standard process.

7.1.2 Review and Approval: Batch Records must be reviewed for accuracy and completeness by a qualified individual. Upon verification, the records should be approved and signed off by the designated authority.

7.2 Logs

7.2.1 Maintenance: Logs must be maintained for all critical equipment and processes. This includes, but is not limited to, cleaning logs, maintenance logs, calibration logs, and temperature control logs.

7.2.2 Details: Each log entry must include the date, time, details of the activity (e.g., equipment used, maintenance performed), and the name of the individual(s) who carried out the activity.

7.3 Retention Policy

7.3.1 Duration: All Batch Records and Logs must be retained for a minimum of five years from the date of creation. This ensures that there is a traceable history of production and quality control activities.

7.3.2 Storage: Documents must be stored in a manner that ensures they are protected from damage, alteration, and unauthorized access. Both physical and electronic documents should be stored securely, with electronic records backed up in a separate location.

7.3.3 Accessibility: During the retention period, Batch Records and Logs must be readily accessible for review by authorized personnel and regulatory inspectors. Appropriate measures must be taken to ensure confidentiality is maintained while allowing for necessary access.

7.3.4 Disposal: At the end of the retention period, documents may be disposed of in a secure and confidential manner. A record of the disposal, including the date and method of destruction, must be maintained.

These Documentation Requirements are designed to ensure the integrity, reliability, and traceability of our production processes and quality control measures. Compliance with these requirements is mandatory for all employees and is subject to regular audits and reviews to ensure ongoing adherence.
