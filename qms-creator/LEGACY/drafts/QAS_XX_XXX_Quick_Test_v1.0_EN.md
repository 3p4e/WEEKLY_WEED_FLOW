### 4. PROCEDURE

#### 4.1 Prerequisites

Before initiating the Quick Test, personnel must ensure the following prerequisites are met:

**Training:**
- All personnel involved must have completed the General GMP training module, which lasts for approximately 1 hour. This training is crucial for understanding the basic Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) applicable across all operations.

**Equipment:**
- As per the nature of the Quick Test, specific equipment requirements are not delineated. However, it is implicit that standard laboratory equipment relevant to conducting tests should be in operational condition and calibrated as per the standard operating procedures.

**Safety:**
- Although no specific Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is mandated, it is recommended that standard laboratory safety protocols be followed. This includes the use of lab coats, gloves, and safety glasses as a minimum requirement to ensure the safety of personnel.

#### 4.2 Process Steps

1. **Preparation:**
   - Ensure all necessary equipment is clean, calibrated, and ready for use.
   - Verify that the testing environment is appropriately prepared according to the standard laboratory conditions required for the Quick Test.

2. **Sample Collection:**
   - Collect the sample to be tested as per the standard sample collection procedures ensuring no contamination occurs during the process.

3. **Test Setup:**
   - Set up the test according to the Quick Test guidelines ensuring all reagents and equipment are within reach and are at the required conditions for the test.

4. **Conducting the Test:**
   - Follow the specific steps outlined in the Quick Test protocol, carefully measuring and adding any reagents as required.
   - Ensure that the test is initiated under the conditions specified (e.g., temperature, time).

5. **Observation and Recording:**
   - Observe the test progression as per the protocol, recording all relevant data immediately to ensure accuracy.

6. **Result Interpretation:**
   - Upon completion of the test, interpret the results following the guidelines provided within the Quick Test protocol.

7. **Post-Test Clean-Up:**
   - Properly dispose of any test reagents, samples, or materials used in accordance with safety and environmental protocols.
   - Clean and store equipment as per the laboratory standard operating procedures.

#### 4.3 In-Process Controls

During the Quick Test, the following in-process controls are to be observed:

- **Sample Integrity:** Ensure the sample remains uncontaminated throughout the testing process.
- **Reagent Validity:** Verify reagents are within their expiration date and stored under correct conditions prior to use.
- **Environmental Conditions:** Monitor and maintain the required environmental conditions (e.g., temperature, humidity) as specified for the test.
- **Data Accuracy:** Ensure all data collected during the test is accurate and recorded promptly.

#### 4.4 Completion Criteria

The Quick Test is considered complete when:

- All steps outlined in section 4.2 have been followed without deviation.
- In-process controls specified in section 4.3 have been satisfied.
- Test results have been accurately recorded, interpreted, and documented as per the documentation requirements.
- All used materials, reagents, and samples have been disposed of or stored correctly, and equipment has been cleaned and stored as per laboratory protocols.

Upon completion, the results should be reviewed and approved by authorized personnel before being reported or used for further processing. The retention period for all test documentation is 5 years, ensuring compliance with the prescribed documentation requirements.

### 7. DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

The documentation requirements are essential for ensuring the traceability, accountability, and quality of processes. These requirements are designed to ensure that all necessary documentation is accurately maintained, readily available, and securely stored. The key components of the documentation requirements include Batch Records, Logs, and a Retention Policy.

#### 7.1 Batch Records

Batch Records are comprehensive documents that provide a detailed account of the production process for each batch of product manufactured. These records must include, but are not limited to, the following information:

- Product name and batch identification number
- Date(s) of manufacture
- Detailed step-by-step records of the production process
- Names of all personnel involved in the production
- Records of all raw materials used, including their quantities, lot numbers, and suppliers
- Results of in-process controls and any adjustments made during production
- Final yield, including a record of any deviations or anomalies encountered during the manufacturing process
- Quality control release results

#### 7.2 Logs

Logs must be maintained to record critical information relevant to operations, equipment, cleaning, and personnel. These logs serve as an ongoing record to demonstrate compliance with established procedures and to ensure the traceability of actions taken. Logs should include, but are not limited to:

- Equipment use and maintenance logs, detailing the date of use, user, operation conducted, any maintenance or calibration performed, and any anomalies or malfunctions noted.
- Cleaning logs, recording dates, personnel, methods, and agents used for cleaning, along with any test results to confirm cleanliness.
- Training logs for personnel, indicating the nature of the training, dates, and outcomes.

#### 7.3 Retention Policy

All documentation, including Batch Records and Logs, must be retained for a minimum period of 5 years from the date of manufacture or as otherwise mandated by regulatory requirements. The retention policy must ensure that:

- Documents are stored in a manner that protects them from damage, deterioration, or loss.
- Documentation is accessible for inspection or audit purposes during the retention period.
- A secure method of destruction is employed after the retention period has expired, ensuring that confidential information is irretrievably destroyed.

The adherence to these documentation requirements is critical for compliance with regulatory standards, ensuring product quality and safety, and for the effective management of manufacturing processes. Any deviations from these requirements must be documented and justified.
