# 1. PURPOSE

The purpose of this Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) `EQU_06.99` titled "Test Equipment Calibration SOP" is to define the systematic process required for the calibration of test equipment used within the Quality Control (QC) and maintenance departments of all relevant facilities handling cannabis products. This SOP is intended to ensure that all test equipment operates within specified limits, providing accurate and reliable results that are critical for maintaining the quality, safety, and efficacy of cannabis products.

This procedure outlines the responsibilities, calibration frequency, methods, documentation, and corrective actions for equipment found to be out of calibration. The adherence to this SOP supports the organization’s commitment to comply with EudraLex Volume 4 - EU GMP, EudraLex Annex 15 - Qualification and Validation, WHO GMP Guidelines, Good Agricultural and Collection Practices (GACP), and ICH Q7 - GMP for APIs, ensuring that all calibration activities meet regulatory requirements and industry best practices.

The implementation of this SOP is a key element of the Quality Management System (QMS), demonstrating the organization's dedication to the integrity of the cannabis production process and the assurance of product quality and safety. The standardized calibration process outlined herein is designed to mitigate risks associated with the use of improperly calibrated equipment, thereby enhancing the overall quality control measures and ensuring compliance with the applicable regulatory standards.

Target Audience:
- QC Personnel
- Maintenance Staff

By ensuring the precise calibration of test equipment, this SOP directly supports the achievement of Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs) and Critical Process Parameters (CPPs), although not explicitly mentioned within the quality requirements, and upholds the integrity of in-process controls, contributing to the consistent production of high-quality cannabis products.

### 2. SCOPE

#### 2.1 Applicability

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) applies to all Quality Control (QC) Personnel and Maintenance Staff engaged in the calibration of test equipment within all relevant facilities of the organization. It is designed to ensure a consistent and compliant approach to the calibration of test equipment, which is critical for maintaining the accuracy and reliability of measurements in the Quality Department.

#### 2.2 Covered Activities

The SOP encompasses a range of activities related to the calibration of test equipment, including but not limited to:

- The scheduling of calibration activities to ensure timely execution.
- The selection and qualification of standards and reference materials used in calibration.
- The execution of calibration procedures, including adjustments, verifications, and re-calibrations.
- The documentation and recording of calibration results and any corrective actions taken.
- The handling of equipment found to be out of calibration, including the assessment of the impact on previously generated data.

#### 2.3 Exclusions

There are no exclusions. This SOP is applicable to all test equipment calibration activities conducted within the specified departments and areas of the organization.

## 4. PROCEDURE

### 4.1 Prerequisites

#### 4.1.1 Training
- All personnel involved in the calibration of test equipment must have completed the General GMP training module, lasting a minimum of 1 hour.
- Only qualified QC Personnel and Maintenance Staff are authorized to perform or oversee the calibration procedures.

#### 4.1.2 Equipment
- Ensure all necessary calibration instruments and test equipment are available and have been verified for accuracy against certified standards before commencing calibration activities.
- A list of required equipment includes, but is not limited to, digital multimeters, pressure gauges, temperature simulators, and weight sets.

#### 4.1.3 Safety
- While personal protective equipment (PPE) is not specifically required for calibration activities, adherence to general laboratory safety protocols is mandatory.
- Identify and mitigate any potential hazards associated with the calibration process prior to beginning work.

### 4.2 Process Steps

1. **Preparation and Initial Assessment**
   - Verify the calibration status and service history of the equipment to be calibrated.
   - Ensure that the environment is suitable for calibration activities (e.g., appropriate temperature and humidity levels).

2. **Instrumentation Setup**
   - Set up calibration instruments and test equipment according to the manufacturer's guidelines and SOP specifications.
   - Ensure that all connections are secure to avoid measurement errors.

3. **Performance Verification and Adjustment**
   - Conduct initial measurements to ascertain the current performance of the equipment.
   - Compare these measurements against the equipment's predefined specifications or acceptance criteria.
   - If the equipment does not meet specifications, adjust or service it as required and then retest to ensure compliance.

4. **Record Keeping**
   - Document all calibration activities, including initial measurements, adjustments made, final outcomes, and any anomalies observed during the process.
   - Ensure records are clear, legible, and stored securely for a minimum retention period of 5 years.

5. **Post-Calibration Verification**
   - Perform a final set of measurements to verify that the equipment is operating within its specified parameters after any adjustments.
   - Confirm that all calibrated equipment is labeled with the calibration status and the next due date for calibration.

6. **Return to Service**
   - Once calibration is verified, and compliance is documented, return the equipment to its designated operational status.

### 4.3 In-Process Controls

- **During Preparation:** Confirm the calibration due dates and review the equipment's service history to ensure no recurring issues could affect calibration accuracy.
- **During Instrumentation Setup:** Regularly check the calibration standards and instruments for any signs of malfunction or drift.
- **During Performance Verification:** Continuously monitor the environmental conditions, ensuring they remain within acceptable limits to prevent impact on calibration accuracy.
- **Post-Calibration Verification:** Double-check all measurement data and ensure consistency before finalizing the calibration process.

### 4.4 Completion Criteria

- The equipment must meet or exceed all specified performance criteria according to the manufacturer's specifications and regulatory standards.
- Calibration records must be complete, accurate, and filed accordingly with the equipment's maintenance history.
- The calibrated equipment must be clearly labeled with the date of calibration, the name of the individual who performed the calibration, and the due date for the next calibration.
- All calibration procedures and outcomes must be reviewed and approved by a designated Quality Control supervisor to ensure compliance with this SOP and regulatory guidelines.

7. DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

The Documentation Requirements section outlines the necessary documents and records that must be maintained to ensure compliance, traceability, and quality control throughout the processes. This includes the creation, maintenance, and retention of Batch Records, Logs, and adherence to a defined Retention Policy.

7.1 Batch Records

Batch Records are critical documents that provide detailed accounts of the production process for each batch of product manufactured. These records must include, but are not limited to, the following information:

- Batch identification number
- Date of production
- Detailed step-by-step processing instructions followed
- Raw materials used, including amounts, lot numbers, and suppliers
- Processing parameters (e.g., temperatures, times, equipment settings)
- Quality control checks and results
- Any deviations from the standard process, including corrective actions taken
- Final yield with reconciliation
- Signature of the person(s) responsible for the batch production and quality checks

Batch Records ensure traceability of each product batch and are critical for investigating any quality issues that may arise.

7.2 Logs

Logs are to be kept for various operational and maintenance activities to ensure that all processes and equipment are operating as intended and are in compliance with regulatory standards. Logs should be maintained for, but not limited to:

- Equipment use and maintenance
- Calibration records
- Cleaning schedules and records
- Environmental monitoring (e.g., temperature, humidity)
- Personnel training and qualifications
- Safety inspections

Each entry in a log must be dated, detail the specific activity performed, the outcome, and be signed by the individual responsible for the activity.

7.3 Retention Policy

All Batch Records, Logs, and related documentation must be retained for a minimum of 5 years after the date of distribution of the product. This Retention Policy is designed to ensure that all necessary documentation is available for the required period for regulatory inspections, audits, and any potential investigations into product quality or safety issues.

The following points must be observed in relation to the Retention Policy:

- Documents should be stored in a manner that ensures they are protected from physical damage, deterioration, or loss.
- Electronic records must be backed up and stored in a secure location with restricted access to safeguard against unauthorized alterations.
- A documented procedure for the retrieval of records must be established to ensure that any document can be efficiently accessed when required.

Adherence to these Documentation Requirements is essential for ensuring regulatory compliance, supporting quality control processes, and facilitating continuous improvement efforts within the organization.

### 8. TRAINING REQUIREMENTS

For the implementation and adherence to the Test Equipment Calibration Standard Operating Procedure (SOP), the following training requirements have been established to ensure all personnel are competent and capable of performing their duties effectively. These requirements are divided into initial training for new employees or employees new to the process, and retraining for existing employees to maintain high standards of proficiency.

#### 8.1 Initial Training

1. **Introduction to Calibration Principles:** Employees must undergo training that covers the fundamental principles of calibration, including definitions, importance, and the impact of calibration on quality and safety.
   
2. **Overview of Test Equipment Calibration SOP:** Detailed walkthrough of the SOP, focusing on the scope, purpose, responsibilities, and procedural steps. This will include the documentation process, frequency of calibrations, handling of equipment, and corrective actions for non-conformances.

3. **Hands-on Training:** Practical training sessions where employees are taught how to perform calibrations according to the SOP. This includes the use of calibration equipment, interpretation of results, and adjustment techniques.

4. **Documentation and Record Keeping:** Instructions on how to accurately complete calibration records, logs, and any associated paperwork as per the SOP requirements.

5. **Quality Assurance and Compliance:** Training on the quality standards and regulatory requirements related to test equipment calibration, highlighting the role of calibration in maintaining compliance.

6. **Safety Training:** Specific safety procedures and precautions associated with handling test equipment and performing calibration tasks.

#### 8.2 Retraining

1. **Annual Refresher Courses:** All employees involved in the calibration process must undergo annual refresher training to ensure they remain up-to-date with the SOP, any changes to calibration procedures, and best practices in equipment calibration.

2. **Post-Revision Training:** Whenever the Test Equipment Calibration SOP is revised or updated, all relevant personnel must receive training on the changes and how they affect the calibration process.

3. **Remedial Training:** If deficiencies are identified during internal audits, performance evaluations, or as a result of non-conformances, affected employees must undergo remedial training to address specific gaps in knowledge or skills.

4. **Technology and Equipment Updates:** Training must be provided when new technology or equipment is introduced to the calibration process. This ensures that personnel are competent in the use of the latest tools and methodologies.

#### Training Documentation

All training activities must be documented, including the date of training, topics covered, names of the instructor(s), and attendees. Employees must demonstrate competency in the SOP and related tasks to successfully complete the training. Documentation of training will be maintained by the Quality Assurance department and will be subject to review during internal and external audits.

#### Competency Assessment

Following initial training and subsequent retraining, employees' competencies should be assessed through practical demonstrations, written tests, or a combination of both. This is to ensure that the training has been effective and that all personnel can perform their roles in accordance with the SOP.

By adhering to these training requirements, the organization will ensure that all personnel involved in the calibration of test equipment are competent, capable, and contributing to the overall quality and integrity of the calibration process.
