```markdown
# 1. PURPOSE

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP), document number QA_00.16, titled "Labeling and Label Control," is established to define the systematic process for the labeling of cannabis products and the control of such labels within all relevant facilities under the purview of the Quality Department. This document is designed to ensure that all labeling activities are conducted in accordance with the principles of Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) and are consistent across all operations to maintain product integrity, traceability, and compliance with regulatory requirements.

The primary objectives of this SOP are to:

- Ensure the accurate, clear, and consistent labeling of cannabis products, thereby supporting product identity, potency, quality, and safety.
- Establish controls for the approval, issuance, and reconciliation of labels and labeling materials to prevent mix-ups, mislabeling, and other errors.
- Outline the responsibilities of personnel involved in labeling activities to ensure accountability and adherence to quality standards.
- Detail the procedures for handling deviations in labeling processes, including the documentation and investigation of such deviations.
- Provide guidance on the training requirements for personnel involved in labeling operations to ensure competency and compliance with established procedures.
- Set forth the documentation and record-keeping practices necessary for traceability, auditability, and compliance with regulatory expectations regarding labeling and label control.

This SOP supports the Quality Management System (QMS) by ensuring that Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs), Critical Process Parameters (CPPs), and in-process controls are managed effectively during the labeling process. It further ensures that equipment required for labeling operations is specified and that generated batch records and electronic records are maintained for a retention period of the product shelf life plus one year. This document also outlines the need for specialized training and practical demonstrations for personnel involved in labeling activities, emphasizing the GMP critical nature of these processes.

By adhering to the procedures described in this SOP, the organization commits to upholding the highest standards of quality and regulatory compliance in the labeling and label control of cannabis products, thereby ensuring the safety, efficacy, and satisfaction of end-users.
```

### 4. PROCEDURE

#### 4.1 Prerequisites

Before commencing the labeling and label control operations, ensure the following prerequisites are adequately met to guarantee compliance and efficiency in the process:

1. **Training**: All personnel involved in the labeling operations must have completed specialized training modules, including a 1-hour General GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) course, and demonstrated practical competency in handling labeling equipment and understanding label control processes.
  
2. **Equipment**: Ensure all required labeling equipment is available, calibrated, and has undergone preventive maintenance check-ups as per the equipment SOPs. This includes labeling machines, printers for batch codes, and inspection systems for label verification.

3. **Safety**: While personal protective equipment (PPE) is not explicitly required for labeling operations, adherence to general safety guidelines within the production area is mandatory. Ensure emergency procedures are known to all personnel.

#### 4.2 Process Steps

To maintain the integrity and compliance of the labeling process, follow these sequenced steps, paying close attention to critical process parameters (CPPs) and critical quality attributes (CQAs):

1. **Preparation of Labeling Materials**:
   - Verify that the labels and packaging materials correspond to the product and batch being processed. This includes checking the product name, strength, dosage form, and batch number.
   - Inspect the physical condition of labels to ensure they are free from tears, smudges, or misprints.

2. **Label Printing and Verification**:
   - Print batch-specific information, including but not limited to the batch number, manufacturing date, and expiry date, ensuring the print is clear and legible.
   - Perform a first-piece verification against the master label to confirm correctness of all printed information. Document the verification.

3. **Label Application**:
   - Set up the labeling machine according to the product specification, adjusting the label position, speed, and application method as necessary.
   - Apply the first label and inspect alignment, adhesion, and quality. Adjust machine settings if required before proceeding with full batch labeling.

4. **In-Line Quality Checks**:
   - Conduct random inspections of labeled products at defined intervals to verify label placement, legibility of printed information, and adhesive quality.
   - Document each inspection, and quarantine any units found non-compliant for further review.

5. **Labeling Completion and Batch Record Documentation**:
   - Once labeling is completed, perform a final inspection of the batch to ensure all units are correctly labeled and that there are no deviations from the SOP.
   - Update the batch records with details of the labeling process, including quantities labeled, inspection results, and any deviations encountered.

#### 4.3 In-Process Controls

Throughout the labeling operation, perform the following in-process controls to ensure continuous compliance and quality:

- **Verification of Label Information**: Before and during the labeling process, verify the accuracy of all label information against the product specifications and batch records.
  
- **Label Adhesion Test**: Periodically, perform adhesion tests to ensure labels remain affixed under expected conditions of storage and transport.

- **Inspection Frequency**: Set inspection frequencies based on historical data, with a minimum of one inspection every hour of operation or per every 1000 units labeled, whichever comes first.

#### 4.4 Completion Criteria

The labeling and label control operation is considered complete when the following criteria are met:

- All units of the batch have been inspected and found compliant with the labeling specifications.
- Batch records have been fully updated with labeling details, inspection results, and any deviations, with corrective actions documented where applicable.
- A final review and sign-off by the Quality Assurance department have been obtained, confirming that the labeling operation complies with GMP standards and the specifics outlined in this SOP.

Upon successful completion, proceed to the next stage in the production process, ensuring all associated documentation is transferred alongside the batch for further processing or packaging.

### 7. DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

Proper documentation is essential for maintaining high-quality standards, ensuring traceability, and fulfilling regulatory compliance. The following documentation requirements are designed to establish a comprehensive and transparent record-keeping system.

#### 7.1 Batch Records

Batch records are a critical component of the documentation system. They provide a detailed account of the production process for each batch, including but not limited to:

- **Product Identification:** Name, batch number, and any other identifiers.
- **Raw Materials:** List of all materials used, including quantities, lot numbers, and suppliers.
- **Production Process:** Detailed step-by-step account of the production process, including times, temperatures, and equipment used.
- **Quality Control Tests:** Results of all in-process and final quality control tests.
- **Packaging Details:** Information on the packaging process and materials used.
- **Release Information:** Details on the batch release, including date of release and the personnel responsible.

#### 7.2 Logs

Logs must be maintained to record various activities and parameters associated with the manufacturing environment and processes. These include, but are not limited to:

- **Equipment Use Log:** Record of all equipment used, including dates, times, and any maintenance activities.
- **Environmental Monitoring Log:** Details of environmental conditions, such as temperature and humidity, especially if critical to product quality.
- **Cleaning Log:** Record of cleaning activities, including what was cleaned, by whom, and when.
- **Training Log:** Records of staff training on processes, procedures, and equipment.

#### 7.3 Retention Policy

All documentation, including batch records and logs, must be retained for a minimum of five years. This retention period supports the need for traceability in case of product recalls, audits, or investigations. The retention policy must ensure that:

- **Accessibility:** Documents must be stored in a manner that allows for easy retrieval during the retention period.
- **Security:** Measures must be in place to protect against unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction of records.
- **Format:** Consideration must be given to the format of storage, ensuring that electronic records are backed up and that paper records are protected from damage.

#### 7.4 Compliance

All documentation practices must comply with relevant regulatory requirements and standards. It is the responsibility of the organization to stay updated with any changes in regulations and to ensure that documentation practices are adjusted accordingly.

#### 7.5 Training

Personnel involved in creating, managing, or using documentation must be adequately trained. Training should cover the importance of documentation, the specific documentation practices of the organization, and any regulatory requirements related to documentation.

#### 7.6 Audit and Review

Regular audits should be conducted to ensure compliance with the documentation requirements. Findings from audits and reviews should be used to continuously improve documentation practices.

By adhering to these documentation requirements, the organization ensures that its products are manufactured consistently to the required standards and that it remains in compliance with regulatory expectations.

### 8. TRAINING REQUIREMENTS

For the efficient implementation of "Labeling and Label Control" processes, comprehensive training programs must be established for all personnel involved in the labeling operations. These programs are designed to ensure that employees are knowledgeable and competent in handling labeling tasks in compliance with regulatory requirements and industry best practices. The training requirements are categorized into initial training and retraining.

#### 8.1 Initial Training

Initial training is mandatory for all new employees or employees taking on new responsibilities related to labeling and label control. This training will cover the following areas:

- **Introduction to Labeling Standards and Regulations:** An overview of the applicable regulatory requirements and standards for labeling in the specific industry (e.g., pharmaceuticals, food production, cosmetics). This will include national and international regulations as applicable.

- **Label Design and Specifications:** Training on the design aspects of labels, including layout, mandatory information, font sizes, and the inclusion of barcodes or QR codes. This will also cover the selection of label materials and adhesives appropriate for different products and environments.

- **Labeling Procedures and Documentation:** Detailed instructions on the standard operating procedures (SOPs) for labeling operations. This includes label generation, printing, application, inspection, and record-keeping.

- **Quality Control in Labeling:** Education on the quality control measures and validation processes to ensure the accuracy and integrity of labels. This includes training on how to perform label inspections and handle non-conformances.

- **Health and Safety:** Guidance on the health and safety practices related to labeling operations, including handling of labeling machinery and materials.

#### 8.2 Retraining

Retraining is essential to maintain a high level of competency and to update employees on any changes in regulatory requirements, labeling technology, and internal procedures. Retraining should be conducted:

- **Annually:** To refresh knowledge and skills in labeling and label control as part of the continuous professional development of the staff.

- **Upon Regulation or Process Changes:** Whenever there are updates to regulations, standards, or internal SOPs related to labeling, affected employees must undergo retraining to understand and implement these changes.

- **Post-Incident:** Following any labeling errors, deviations, or quality issues, involved personnel should receive targeted retraining to prevent recurrence. This retraining should focus on the root cause of the incident and measures for corrective action.

#### Training Documentation

All training activities must be documented, including the training date, content, trainer, and attendees. Employees' competency and understanding should be assessed at the end of each training session, with records maintained for audit purposes.

By adhering to these training requirements, organizations will ensure that their labeling and label control processes are performed by knowledgeable and skilled personnel, thereby minimizing the risk of errors, ensuring regulatory compliance, and safeguarding product integrity.
