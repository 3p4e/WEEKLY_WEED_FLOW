4. PROCEDURE

4.1 Prerequisites

4.1.1 Training: All personnel involved in the Cannabis Cultivation Trimming Process should have completed the General GMP training module of 1 hour. This will ensure familiarity with basic Good Manufacturing Practices essential for maintaining product quality and safety.

4.1.2 Equipment: The required equipment includes specialized cannabis trimming scissors or machines, gloves, and appropriate containers for storing trimmed cannabis. Ensure all equipment is clean and in good working condition before starting the trimming process.

4.1.3 Safety: Personnel must wear the prescribed Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), including gloves, masks, and protective eyewear, to prevent any contamination and ensure safety during the trimming process.

4.2 Process Steps

1. **Preparation**: Ensure that all trimming equipment and work surfaces are sanitized. Wear the required PPE to maintain hygiene and safety standards.

2. **Inspection**: Examine cannabis plants for maturity. Plants ready for trimming typically have brown pistils that curl and a sticky surface.

3. **Harvesting**: Cut the cannabis plants at the base, and transport them to the trimming area in clean, designated containers.

4. **Bud Trimming**:
   a. Separate the buds from branches.
   b. Using the trimming scissors or machine, carefully trim away the sugar leaves without cutting into the bud.
   c. Sort the trimmed buds by size or grade, as applicable.

5. **Quality Check**: Inspect the trimmed buds for consistency in size and quality. Remove any remaining leaves that could affect the quality or appearance.

6. **Drying and Curing** (if part of the trimming process): Place the trimmed buds in a dry, ventilated area or use specialized equipment to dry them until the desired moisture content is reached. Cure the buds if required by the product specifications.

7. **Packaging**: Once drying and curing are completed, if applicable, package the trimmed buds in clean, labeled containers for further processing or sale.

8. **Cleanup**: Clean and sanitize all equipment and work surfaces post-trimming to prevent contamination.

4.3 In-Process Controls

- **Inspection of Equipment**: Prior to use, ensure all trimming equipment is clean and functional. Check for any signs of wear or damage.

- **Quality Checks**: During the trimming process, continuously inspect the buds for uniformity in trimming and remove any leaves or stems that could affect quality. After trimming, a final quality check is necessary to ensure product consistency.

- **Hygiene Monitoring**: Regularly monitor the use of PPE and adherence to hygiene practices among personnel to prevent product contamination.

4.4 Completion Criteria

The Cannabis Cultivation Trimming Process is considered complete when all the harvested cannabis has been trimmed according to the specified quality standards, without any damage to the buds. The trimmed cannabis must be properly dried, cured (if applicable), and packaged in labeled containers ready for further processing, testing, or sale. All equipment and work areas should be cleaned and sanitized, ready for the next batch. The process should be documented and records maintained as per the retention period of 5 years to ensure traceability and compliance.

### 7. DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

Documentation plays a pivotal role in ensuring the quality, traceability, and compliance of processes and products. It is essential for maintaining the integrity of operations and facilitating audits and inspections. The following components outline the essential documentation requirements:

#### 7.1 Batch Records

Batch Records are comprehensive documents that record all details pertinent to the production of a batch of a product. These records must include but are not limited to:

- **Description of Product**: Including the product name, strength, and batch size.
- **Batch Number Identification**: A unique identifier for each batch produced.
- **Manufacturing Date**: The date on which the batch production commenced.
- **Expiry Date**: The date until which the product is deemed safe and effective to use.
- **Raw Material Information**: Details of all raw materials used, including quantities, batch numbers, and suppliers.
- **Manufacturing Process**: A step-by-step account of the manufacturing process, including parameters, equipment used, and in-process controls.
- **Quality Control Results**: Results of testing performed at various stages of production, including in-process, intermediate, and finished product testing.
- **Packaging Details**: Information on packaging materials and processes.
- **Release Information**: Details of the final inspection, testing results, and release decision by the quality control department.

#### 7.2 Logs

Logs should be maintained for various operational, maintenance, and calibration activities. These include but are not limited to:

- **Equipment Use Log**: Documenting the use of each piece of equipment, including dates, times, product batches, and any anomalies observed.
- **Maintenance and Calibration Logs**: Recording all maintenance and calibration activities performed on equipment, including dates, nature of work, and the personnel involved.
- **Cleaning Logs**: Documenting the cleaning of equipment and production areas, including dates, methods used, and personnel involved.
- **Training Logs**: Keeping track of employee training on operations, safety, quality control, and emergency procedures.

#### 7.3 Retention Policy

All documentation, including Batch Records, Logs, and associated documents, must be retained for a minimum of five years. This retention period facilitates the reconstruction of the history of a batch, should the need arise, and supports regulatory inspections and audits. The retention policy should outline the following:

- **Storage Conditions**: Ensuring documents are stored in a manner that preserves their readability, integrity, and accessibility. This may involve physical or electronic storage solutions with appropriate security measures.
- **Retrieval Process**: Establishing a clear process for the retrieval of documents, ensuring that they can be accessed promptly when required.
- **Disposal Procedures**: Defining the methods and conditions under which documents can be securely disposed of after the retention period has ended, ensuring confidentiality and compliance with regulatory requirements.

The adherence to these documentation requirements is essential for compliance with regulatory standards, ensuring product quality and safety, and maintaining operational efficiency.

### 8. TRAINING REQUIREMENTS 

#### For Cannabis Cultivation Trimming Process

The Cannabis Cultivation Trimming Process requires meticulous attention to detail, adherence to compliance standards, and a thorough understanding of the plant's biology. Therefore, to ensure high-quality production, workers involved in trimming must undergo comprehensive training. This section outlines the initial training and retraining requirements necessary for personnel engaged in this critical process.

#### 8.1 Initial Training

Initial training is designed for new employees or those who are transitioning into the trimming role. This training module aims to equip them with the foundational knowledge and skills required for effective performance.

1. **Introduction to Cannabis Cultivation**: Understanding the lifecycle of cannabis, strains, and the significance of trimming in the cultivation process.
2. **Compliance and Safety**: Training on state and federal regulations, workplace safety protocols, personal protective equipment (PPE) usage, and emergency procedures.
3. **Tools and Equipment Handling**: Instruction on the correct use, maintenance, and safety procedures for trimming tools and equipment.
4. **Trimming Techniques**: Detailed training on various trimming techniques, focusing on efficiency and quality. This includes wet trimming, dry trimming, and the nuances of handling different strains.
5. **Quality Control**: Guidelines on identifying quality issues, handling plant diseases, and ensuring the final product meets set quality standards.
6. **Waste Disposal**: Proper methods for disposing of cannabis waste in compliance with regulations.
7. **Record Keeping**: Procedures for maintaining accurate records of trimmed product, waste, and compliance-related documentation.

#### 8.2 Retraining

Retraining ensures that trimming personnel are updated on the latest techniques, compliance changes, and safety protocols. Retraining should be conducted:

1. **Annually**: To refresh employees' knowledge and skills and introduce new standards or technologies in the cultivation and trimming process.
2. **Post-Incident**: Following any safety incident, equipment mishap, or compliance violation to prevent future occurrences.
3. **Upon Regulation Update**: Whenever there are significant updates to state or federal cannabis cultivation regulations.
4. **Technology or Process Change**: When new trimming technologies are introduced or when there is a significant change in the trimming process.

Retraining sessions should be interactive, allowing for Q&A, hands-on demonstrations, and feedback to ensure comprehension and competence.
