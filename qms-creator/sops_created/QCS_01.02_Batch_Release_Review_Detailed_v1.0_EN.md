## 1. PURPOSE

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the systematic framework for comprehensive batch record review and Qualified Person (QP) certification of all finished medicinal cannabis products manufactured at [FACILITY_NAME]. The procedure ensures that each batch of finished product has been manufactured and inspected in compliance with:

- The laws in force in North Macedonia and destination Member States
- The requirements of the Marketing Authorisation (MA)
- Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) for medicinal products per EU GMP Parts I and II, and Annexes 7, 9, 11, and 16

The procedure implements all 21 requirements enumerated in EU GMP Annex 16, Section 1.7, which define the specific duties and responsibilities of the Qualified Person. Through systematic four-phase batch record review (Documentation Compilation → Initial QA Review → Departmental Approvals → QP Certification), the procedure provides the quality assurance framework that allows the QP to take personal responsibility for batch quality and safety prior to release to saleable stock and commercial distribution.

## 3. RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

### 3.1 **Qualified Person (QP)**

**Specific Authorities and Responsibilities:**

The Qualified Person holds the ultimate responsibility for batch certification and must:

#### 3.1.1 Personal Verification Authority
- Personally verify that the batch record review has been completed comprehensively
- Personally review evidence that all 21 Annex 16 requirements have been fulfilled
- Personally review batch test results and acceptance criteria compliance
- Personally assess whether any deviations or investigations preclude certification
- Personally evaluate supply chain verification completeness
- **May NOT delegate the final certification decision**
- **May rely on quality system elements** but retains personal accountability for verification extent

#### 3.1.2 Pre-Certification Verification
- Review the completed Batch Record Review Checklist (Annex A01) prepared by QA
- Request additional information or investigation if batch record completeness is questioned
- Request re-analysis or additional testing if result accuracy is uncertain
- Verify that all Annex 16 requirements have been personally assessed
- Ensure all departmental approvals (Production, QC, QA) are documented and valid

#### 3.1.3 Certification Decision and Authority
- **APPROVE AND RELEASE**: Issue formal QP Certification Statement if all conditions are satisfied
- **REJECT**: Refuse certification if:
  - Any specification failure is identified (non-negotiable)
  - Investigation remains incomplete with unresolved quality status
  - Significant GMP non-compliance is identified
  - Data integrity concerns are present
  - Supply chain cannot be adequately verified
  - Regulatory constraints preclude release
  - Information is insufficient for decision-making
- **CONDITIONAL RELEASE** (with documented justification under exceptional circumstances and documented QRM): Release with specific conditions documented in the batch dossier and QP Certification Statement

#### 3.1.4 Regulatory Accountability
- Sign the QP Certification Statement with personal signature
- Enter certification in the Batch Release Register (Annex A03)
- Take personal accountability for certification decision and its consequences
- Report all batch rejections to the Quality Manager within 24 hours
- Maintain detailed records of all certification decisions
- Cooperate with regulatory inspections regarding batch certifications

#### 3.1.5 Authority Limits
- The QP's decision on batch certification cannot be overridden by any other authority
- However, the Qualified Person may be overridden by competent authority (e.g., national regulatory agency) instruction
- The QP must refuse certification if directed by competent authority or if legal obligation requires rejection

### 3.2 **Head of Quality Assurance**

**Specific Authorities and Responsibilities:**

#### 3.2.1 Batch Record Review Authority
- Authorises and directs the QA Batch Record Reviewer to initiate batch record review
- Personally reviews the completed Batch Record Review Checklist
- Verifies that all discrepancies have been properly documented and resolved
- Makes the formal QA recommendation: **RECOMMEND FOR QP CERTIFICATION** or **DO NOT RECOMMEND**
- Authorises forward to QP or directs return to departments for additional work

#### 3.2.2 Discrepancy Assessment
- Reviews all identified discrepancies (Minor/Major/Critical classification)
- Assesses whether identified defects have been adequately resolved
- Determines whether any defect precludes certification
- Documents the rationale for discrepancy classification in the batch dossier

#### 3.2.3 Documentation Authority
- Verifies that all required batch documentation is present and complete
- Identifies missing or incomplete documentation and directs correction
- Ensures all required signatures and approvals are obtained
- Maintains the Batch Release Register entry

#### 3.2.4 Escalation Authority
- May escalate unresolved deviations or investigations to the Quality Manager
- May request additional investigation before forwarding to QP
- May hold batch in review pending clarification

### 3.3 **Head of Quality Control**

**Specific Authorities and Responsibilities:**

#### 3.3.1 QC Data Review
- Verifies that all required QC tests have been performed
- Reviews Certificate of Analysis (CoA) completeness and accuracy
- Confirms that all test results meet registered specifications
- Identifies any specification excursions or out-of-specification results
- Approves analytical data accuracy and method compliance

#### 3.3.2 Method and Data Validation
- Verifies that analytical methods are appropriately validated
- Confirms that system suitability tests are within acceptance criteria
- Verifies that reference standards are valid (within expiry, appropriate storage)
- Confirms that all equipment is calibrated and functioning correctly
- Reviews all analytical raw data (chromatograms, notebooks, logbooks)

#### 3.3.3 Authorisation Statement
- Signs the Batch Release Authorisation Form (Annex A02) confirming:
  - QC testing completeness
  - Specification compliance
  - Data integrity
  - CoA accuracy and approval

#### 3.3.4 OOS/OOT Investigation Authority
- Takes responsibility for all out-of-specification or out-of-trend investigations
- Decides on re-analysis requirements
- Determines whether assignable cause is established
- Authorises batch rejection or acceptance based on investigation findings

### 3.4 **Head of Production/Cultivation**

**Specific Authorities and Responsibilities:**

#### 3.4.1 Manufacturing Documentation Authority
- Verifies that all required manufacturing batch records are present and complete
- For cultivated products: Confirms GACP cultivation documentation completeness
- Confirms that all critical process parameters (CPPs) were monitored and recorded
- Verifies that environmental monitoring was performed and documented
- Confirms that all equipment used was qualified, calibrated, and functioning

#### 3.4.2 Yield Reconciliation
- Verifies material accountability from starting materials through finished product
- Confirms that yield is within documented and approved limits
- Investigates any unexplained yield variations or material loss
- Signs the Batch Release Authorisation Form confirming manufacturing compliance

#### 3.4.3 Deviation and Investigation Authority
- Takes responsibility for any manufacturing deviations identified during batch review
- Provides documentation of deviation investigation and corrective action
- Confirms that any manufacturing-related OOS results have been investigated
- Authorises any manufacturing-related data corrections or retest

#### 3.4.4 Personnel and Training Verification
- Confirms that all personnel who handled the batch were trained and competent
- Verifies that required competency assessments are documented
- Identifies any training gaps and ensures correction before batch release

### 3.5 **QA Batch Record Reviewer**

**Specific Authorities and Responsibilities:**

#### 3.5.1 Batch Record Compilation
- Collects and verifies completeness of all required batch documentation
- Checks that all required forms are completed and signed
- Verifies that all analytical reports are attached to batch dossier
- Ensures chronological file organisation per ALCOA+ principles

#### 3.5.2 Systematic Checklist Review
- Completes the Batch Record Review Checklist (Annex A01) comprehensively
- Verifies presence of all 100+ required documents and records
- Documents all discrepancies (Minor/Major/Critical) with specific references
- Requests clarification or correction of incomplete items from responsible departments

#### 3.5.3 Data Verification
- Verifies ALCOA+ (Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate, plus Complete, Consistent, Enduring) compliance
- Checks numerical consistency (yield calculations, dates, quantities)
- Identifies any unclear entries or unsigned documents
- Flags any evidence of data modification or correction requiring justification

#### 3.5.4 Recommendation Authority
- Provides preliminary assessment: **SATISFACTORY** or **UNSATISFACTORY**
- Documents all identified deficiencies clearly for departmental correction
- Does NOT make final recommendation—that is the Head of QA responsibility
- Escalates critical findings (specification failures, data integrity concerns) immediately to Head of QA

#### 3.5.5 Delegation Limitations
- This role requires competent personnel with minimum 3 years QA experience
- Must have received specific training on this SOP and batch record review procedures
- May be delegated to trained QA personnel under Head of QA supervision
- Cannot be delegated to any personnel from Production, QC, or external departments

## 5. REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

### 5.1 **Primary Regulatory Authority**

**Directive 2001/83/EC, Article 51: Each batch of finished medicinal product shall, before being released for distribution, be approved by the person responsible for releasing the batch, who must be the Qualified Person**

This directive establishes QP certification as a mandatory legal requirement, not optional or discretionary. No batch may enter distribution without QP approval.

### 5.2 **EU GMP Annex 16 Requirements**

EU GMP Annex 16 Section 1 establishes comprehensive requirements for QP certification. The QP must personally ensure that **all 21 specific duties** have been fulfilled before signing the certification statement.

#### **Section 1.7: The 21 Specific QP Duties**

The Qualified Person is personally responsible for securing compliance with all of the following points before releasing each batch:

| # | Requirement | Verification Method |
|---|------------|-------------------|
| **1.7.1** | **Marketing Authorisation Compliance**: The finished product conforms with the provision of the MA granted by the competent authority | Review MA dossier; compare batch specifications, process, packaging to approved dossier |
| **1.7.2** | **GMP Compliance**: The manufacturing process has been carried out in accordance with the requirements of these guidelines | Review manufacturing BPR; verify adherence to SOP procedures |
| **1.7.3** | **Process Validation**: The process validation is within the approved limits | Verify process validation status per [FACILITY_CODE]-VAL-PRC-001 |
| **1.7.4** | **Quality Control**: Finished product control has been carried out in accordance with the requirements of Part II | Review CoA; verify all required tests completed |
| **1.7.5** | **Specification Testing**: All the results of the analysis of the finished product for compliance with the approved specification are satisfactory | Confirm no OOS results; review all analytical data |
| **1.7.6** | **Supplier Audits**: The opinions or reports of the importer of the finished product, where these exist, have been taken into account | Review all supplier audit reports; confirm any findings resolved |
| **1.7.7** | **Personnel Responsibility**: The person responsible for releasing the batch in the importing Member State (where applicable) has certified that his State allows him to do so | Confirm MIA registration in destination Member State |
| **1.7.8** | **Importation Route (if applicable)**: Where the finished product is imported from a third country, the finished product has been tested in full in accordance with Part II, Chapter 3, unless, exceptionally, and for the final product release, the person responsible in the importing Member State waives certain tests on the basis of the assessment of the starting materials, the process and the controls carried out during manufacture | Verify all required importation testing completed |
| **1.7.9** | **Site Knowledge**: He has ensured that he is in the position of being able to certify that each batch of his manufactur[ed] product has been produced and controlled in accordance with the requirements of the MA and the rules of good manufacturing practice | Confirm personal knowledge of manufacturing process and site |
| **1.7.10** | **Written Procedures**: He has ensured that any calculation, conversion, manipulation of data, and review and approval of data has been done by properly trained and authorised personnel | Verify personnel training and written procedures |
| **1.7.11** | **Contract Arrangements**: Any contract arrangements made between the holder of the manufacturing authorisation and any other organisation must be discussed with and approved by the competent authority in the Member State in which the batch is to be released | Verify contract arrangements approval; review CMO and CRO quality agreements |
| **1.7.12** | **Supply Chain Verification**: The supply chain used has been verified | Complete supply chain diagram; verify all suppliers and sites in chain |
| **1.7.13** | **GDP Compliance (if applicable)**: Where the finished product has been stored in the importing Member State, storage conditions and transport have been in accordance with Good Distribution Practice | Verify GDP compliance of logistics partners |
| **1.7.14** | **Active Substance GMP**: Where a bulk or intermediate has been supplied from a third country, it must have been manufactured at a site which the competent authorities of the Member State of final release recognise as being equivalent to the GMP standards | Verify active substance supplier GMP status |
| **1.7.15** | **Pharmacopoeia Compliance**: The finished product complies with the requirements of the current edition of the European Pharmacopoeia | Verify Ph.Eur. 3028 compliance for cannabis products |
| **1.7.16** | **Reference Standards**: All the reference standards or reference materials used are appropriate (see Chapter 5) | Confirm reference standard validity and traceability |
| **1.7.17** | **Falsified Medicines Measures**: Appropriate measures have been taken to minimise the risk of the batch being a falsified medicinal product, including checking that the manufacturer holds an MIA and that the product and manufacturer are not listed in the FAKEVIGILANS database or equivalent | Verify manufacturer registration; check counterfeiting controls |
| **1.7.18** | **Manufacturing Quality**: Manufacturing quality is such that the batch can be expected to meet the approved specification and intended use requirements | Professional judgment on manufacturing process quality |
| **1.7.19** | **Pharmacological and Toxicological Properties**: The pharmacological and toxicological properties are consistent with previous batches and experience in the market where applicable | Review cannabinoid profiles and safety data consistency |
| **1.7.20** | **Stability Programme**: The batch can be expected to remain in compliance with the specification up to the proposed expiry date when stored under the appropriate storage conditions | Verify stability programme completion; confirm expiry dating |
| **1.7.21** | **Traceability Records**: The batch traceability records can be retrieved and the batch can be readily located in the distribution chain in the event of a recall | Verify seed-to-sale traceability documentation |

## 6. BATCH RELEASE PROCEDURE

The batch release procedure consists of **four sequential phases**, each with defined entrance criteria, activities, and exit criteria. Batches proceed through phases in order and cannot be released until all phases are successfully completed.

### 6.1 **Phase I: Documentation Compilation and Verification**

**Duration:** 1–2 working days
**Responsibility:** QA Batch Record Reviewer + Department Representatives
**Exit Criterion:** Complete and verified batch dossier assembled

#### 6.1.1 **Phase I Activities**

**Step 1: Batch Release Initiation (Day 0–1)**
- QC notifies QA when all testing is complete and CoA is ready for issue
- QA sends batch release notification to all relevant departments (Production/Cultivation, QC, QA)
- QA assigns batch record reviewer and designates batch dossier location
- Batch is placed on hold pending completion of all release activities

**Step 2: Documentation Collection (Day 1–2)**
- QA Batch Record Reviewer collects all required documents from departments:
  - From Production/Cultivation: Manufacturing BPR, cultivation records, environmental monitoring, GACP records, personnel records, training documentation
  - From QC: CoA, analytical worksheets, chromatograms, reference standard records, equipment maintenance logs, method validation status
  - From QA: Deviation reports, OOS investigation reports, change control notices, CAPA reports, validation status, complaints/recalls
- Verification that all required forms are present and properly signed
- Identification of any missing documentation

**Step 3: Preliminary Completeness Check (Day 2)**
- QA Batch Record Reviewer verifies:
  - All required sections of manufacturing BPR are completed and signed
  - All analytical test results are attached to batch dossier
  - All required attachments are present
  - No obviously incomplete fields in critical documents
  - Filing order is logical and retrievable (chronological or by document type)
- Documents are assembled in official batch dossier folder/binder

**Step 4: Verification of Critical Data Points (Day 2)**
- QA Batch Record Reviewer spot-checks critical data consistency:
  - Batch size on manufacturing BPR matches packaging records
  - Dates on manufacturing BPR are sequential and logical
  - Personnel signatures correspond to trained/authorised individuals
  - Batch number is consistent across all documents
  - QC test dates are after manufacturing completion

**Exit Criteria for Phase I:**
- ☑ All required documents are physically present
- ☑ All critical completeness checks are passed
- ☑ Batch dossier is assembled in retrievable format
- ☑ QA Batch Record Reviewer signs Phase I completion statement

#### 6.1.2 **Phase I Exception Handling**

If critical documentation is missing:
- QA Batch Record Reviewer notifies responsible department immediately
- Batch remains on hold
- Missing documentation is retrieved or re-created per original SOP
- Phase I may not proceed to Phase II until all documentation is present

**Note:** Batches cannot proceed to Phase II if Phase I is incomplete.

### 6.2 **Phase II: Initial QA Review and Discrepancy Identification**

**Duration:** 3–5 working days
**Responsibility:** QA Batch Record Reviewer
**Exit Criterion:** Comprehensive batch record review completed with all discrepancies identified and documented

#### 6.2.1 **Phase II Activities**

**Step 1: Comprehensive Batch Record Review Checklist (Days 1–3)**
- QA Batch Record Reviewer completes the Batch Record Review Checklist (Annex A01) systematically
- The checklist comprises **11 major sections** with **100+ individual verification items**:

| Section | Items | Focus Area |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| **A. Batch Identification** | 8 items | Product name, batch number, cannabis strain, THC/CBD content, batch size, dates |
| **B. Cultivation Records (GACP)** | 12 items | Mother plant source, cloning records, cultivation documentation, environmental monitoring, IPM, yield |
| **C. Harvest and Post-Harvest** | 12 items | Harvest records, wet/dry weights, drying parameters, curing, sorting, grading |
| **D. Manufacturing BPR** | 20 items | BPR completeness, equipment use, CPPs, processing steps, IPC results, personnel, material reconciliation |
| **E. Packaging Records** | 18 items | Primary/secondary packaging, label reconciliation, serialisation, anti-tampering measures, storage conditions |
| **F. QC Testing and CoA** | 20 items | Identity testing, potency analysis, contaminant testing (microbial, mycotoxins, pesticides, heavy metals), moisture, appearance |
| **G. Data Integrity (ALCOA+)** | 14 items | Attributability, legibility, contemporaneity, originality, accuracy, completeness, consistency, endurance |
| **H. Deviations and CAPA** | 10 items | Deviation listing, classifications, investigations, corrective actions, effectiveness verification |
| **I. Equipment and Facilities** | 10 items | Equipment qualification, calibration, maintenance, environmental monitoring systems, facility state |
| **J. Personnel and Training** | 6 items | Training records, competency assessments, personnel authorisation levels |
| **K. EU GMP Annex 16 Compliance** | 21 items | Systematic verification of all 21 QP responsibilities from Annex 16 Section 1.7 |

**Step 2: Detailed Item Verification (Days 2–4)**
For each checklist item, QA Batch Record Reviewer:
- Verifies presence of required documentation
- Checks that documentation is complete and properly signed
- Reviews content for accuracy and compliance
- Marks response: **YES** (compliant), **NO** (non-compliant), **N/A** (not applicable)
- Documents any discrepancies with specific reference to document, section, line number
- Classifies discrepancies: **MINOR** (documentation or administrative), **MAJOR** (quality-relevant), **CRITICAL** (potential safety/efficacy impact)

#### 6.2.2 **Discrepancy Classification Guide**

**CRITICAL Discrepancies** (Potential safety/efficacy impact, must be resolved before release):
- Out-of-specification test result not adequately investigated
- Missing critical batch data (e.g., manufacturing dates, test results)
- Significant GMP violation identified in manufacturing documentation
- Data integrity concern (evidence of alteration, missing signatures)
- Equipment qualification or calibration status uncertain
- Material traceability broken or uncertain
- Evidence of unresolved critical deviation

**MAJOR Discrepancies** (Quality system deficiency, should be resolved before release):
- Incomplete specification testing (non-critical test missing)
- Environmental monitoring record incomplete or slightly out-of-range
- Training documentation incomplete for non-critical activity
- Change control procedure not followed for non-critical change
- Minor documentation omissions that can be readily explained

**MINOR Discrepancies** (Administrative or documentation issue, low impact):
- Typographical errors in non-critical fields
- Missing signatures on informational documents
- Incomplete comments sections (not safety-critical)
- Misfiled documents that are nevertheless present
- Administrative forms signed after deadline but before Phase II

**Step 3: Discrepancy Documentation (Days 3–5)**
- QA Batch Record Reviewer documents all identified discrepancies in the **Discrepancy Summary Log** (part of Annex A01):

| Discrepancy # | Description | Classification | Document Reference | Department Responsible | Resolution Status |
|---------------|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|----------------------|------------------|
| 1 | Environmental humidity record for Day 5 illegible | MINOR | Env. Monitoring Log, p. 3 | Production | Clarification provided |
| 2 | Training record for packaging technician Smith dated after batch completion | MAJOR | Training Log, p. 12 | HR/QA | To be resolved before QP review |
| 3 | Cannabinoid analysis SST results not documented | CRITICAL | CoA, Section 3 | QC | Retest completed, new CoA issued |

**Exit Criteria for Phase II:**
- ☑ All 100+ checklist items systematically reviewed
- ☑ All discrepancies identified and documented with references
- ☑ Discrepancies classified appropriately (Critical/Major/Minor)
- ☑ QA Batch Record Reviewer preliminary assessment completed: **SATISFACTORY** or **UNSATISFACTORY**
- ☑ Phase II summary prepared for Head of QA review

#### 6.2.3 **Phase II Exception Handling**

If critical discrepancies are identified:
- QA Batch Record Reviewer immediately notifies Head of QA
- Batch remains in Phase II
- Responsible departments are directed to provide clarification or correction
- Corrected documentation is re-reviewed before proceeding to Phase III

### 6.3 **Phase III: Departmental Review and Authorization**

**Duration:** 2–3 working days
**Responsibility:** Head of Production/Cultivation, Head of QC, Head of QA
**Exit Criterion:** All departmental approvals obtained; formal QA recommendation issued

#### 6.3.1 **Head of Production/Cultivation Review (Day 1–2)**

The Head of Production/Cultivation reviews:
- Manufacturing BPR completeness and accuracy
- Critical process parameter (CPP) conformance
- Yield reconciliation and material accountability
- Environmental monitoring records
- All deviations or discrepancies related to manufacturing
- Resolution of any manufacturing-related discrepancies

**Sign-off Statement by Head of Production:**
"I confirm that the manufacturing operations for this batch have been conducted in accordance with approved procedures, relevant SOPs, and GMP standards. All critical process parameters were within established limits. All manufacturing-related discrepancies have been identified and addressed. Environmental monitoring and GACP cultivation records (where applicable) are complete and compliant. I recommend this batch proceed to QC review."

**Authorisation Options:**
- ☑ **APPROVE** — Forward to QC review
- ☑ **CONDITIONAL APPROVE** — Approve pending minor correction of non-critical item within 5 working days
- ☐ **REJECT** — Return to Phase II for major manufacturing issues

#### 6.3.2 **Head of Quality Control Review (Day 1–2)**

The Head of QC reviews:
- Completeness of all required testing per specification
- Accuracy of Certificate of Analysis (CoA)
- System suitability test (SST) compliance
- Reference standard validity
- Equipment calibration status
- Method validation status
- All analytical raw data (chromatograms, notebooks)
- OOS/OOT investigation completion and conclusions
- Resolution of any QC-related discrepancies

**Sign-off Statement by Head of QC:**
"I confirm that all required quality control testing has been completed in accordance with approved methods and specifications. The Certificate of Analysis accurately reflects all test results. All analytical methods are appropriately validated with current system suitability verification. All reference standards and equipment are within appropriate calibration/expiry. I confirm specification compliance. Any out-of-specification or out-of-trend results have been investigated with documented findings. I recommend this batch proceed to QA final review and QP certification."

**Authorisation Options:**
- ☑ **APPROVE** — Forward to QA final review
- ☑ **CONDITIONAL APPROVE** — Approve pending repeat/confirmation of specific test within 5 working days
- ☐ **REJECT** — Return to Phase II for unresolved analytical issues or specification non-conformance

#### 6.3.3 **Head of Quality Assurance Review (Day 2–3)**

The Head of QA conducts final review before forwarding to QP:
- Reviews the completed Batch Record Review Checklist
- Assesses adequacy of discrepancy resolution by all departments
- Verifies that all critical discrepancies have been satisfactorily addressed
- Makes the **final QA recommendation** (not delegable):

**QA Recommendation Options:**

**OPTION 1: RECOMMEND FOR QP CERTIFICATION**
- All 100+ checklist items are satisfactory
- All critical discrepancies have been resolved
- All departmental approvals are documented
- All Annex 16 requirements can be expected to be fulfilled
- All required documentation is present and complete
- QA recommendation: **"This batch is suitable for QP review and certification"**

**OPTION 2: DO NOT RECOMMEND FOR QP CERTIFICATION**
- Critical unresolved discrepancies exist
- Specification non-conformance has not been adequately explained
- Data integrity concerns remain
- GMP compliance is questionable
- Documentation is incomplete or cannot be verified
- QA recommendation: **"This batch should not proceed to QP certification pending resolution of identified critical issues"**

**OPTION 3: CONDITIONAL RECOMMENDATION FOR QP REVIEW**
- Minor unresolved items exist with documented timeline for resolution
- No critical safety/efficacy issues identified
- QP may review with explicit understanding of pending minor items
- Statement: **"This batch may proceed to QP review pending completion of [specific items] by [specific date]"**

#### 6.3.4 **Phase III Exception Handling**

- That department must document the specific reason (quality issue, missing documentation, etc.)

- Batch may be returned to Phase II for re-review after corrections

**DECISION B: BATCH REJECTED**

The QP must refuse certification if:

**Absolute Barriers to Certification (Non-negotiable):**

| Barrier | Impact | Example |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **OOS Result Not Investigated** | Cannot certify unknown quality status | THC measured 23.5% vs spec NMT 22% with no investigation completed |

| **Incomplete Investigation** | Quality status remains uncertain | Deviation identified but investigation not complete |
| **Significant GMP Violation** | Quality manufacturing not assured | Equipment not qualified; procedure not followed |
| **Data Integrity Concern** | Cannot rely on data; falsification suspected | Signature added after fact; calculation appears manipulated |
| **Supply Chain Cannot Be Verified** | Traceability broken; control uncertain | Supplier not verified; documentation missing |
| **Regulatory Constraint** | Legal obligation to reject | MALMED has issued directive regarding batch |
| **Insufficient Information** | Cannot make informed decision | Critical documentation unavailable; irretrievable loss |

**QP Rejection Statement:**

> *"I, the undersigned Qualified Person, cannot certify batch [BATCH NUMBER] of [PRODUCT NAME] for the following specific reason(s):*
>
> *[Specify the absolute barrier(s) to certification; e.g., "Out-of-specification result for cannabinoid content (THC 23.5% vs specification NMT 22%) has not been adequately investigated"; "Significant deviation in manufacturing procedure identified with impact assessment incomplete"]*
>
> *In accordance with EU GMP Annex 16, Section 1.6, I cannot sign the certification statement because [specific reason]. This batch is REJECTED and cannot be released for distribution."*

**Actions upon rejection:**
- Batch is placed on indefinite hold
- Quality Manager is immediately notified
- Reason for rejection is documented in batch dossier
- Batch is held in secure storage pending further investigation or destruction
- Warehouse is notified of rejection; no shipment authorization is issued
- Customer is notified (if batch was intended for delivery) with regulatory explanation

**DECISION C: BATCH CONDITIONALLY RELEASED (Exceptional Circumstances)**

Under **exceptional circumstances only**, the QP may issue a conditional release with **explicit documented justification** and **Quality Risk Management assessment**:

**Conditions for Conditional Release:**
1. A deviation or investigation conclusion is questionable but the batch meets all specifications
2. The QP conducts a **formal ICH Q9 Quality Risk Management assessment**
3. The QRM assessment concludes that the deviation represents **negligible risk** to product quality/safety/efficacy
4. The QP documents the reasoning explicitly (not just "quality risk management acceptable")
5. The batch record includes the full QRM assessment
6. If applicable, a regulatory variation is submitted to MALMED
7. The QP personally accepts responsibility for the conditional release decision

**Example of Conditional Release Scenario:**
- Environmental humidity exceeded 55% for 6 hours during flowering (specification 30-55%)
- Investigation determines temporary HVAC malfunction was corrected immediately
- Batch testing shows no adverse effects; meets all specifications
- ICH Q9 assessment concludes residual risk is negligible

- Batch record must clearly note conditional release basis

**Conditional Release Statement:**

> *"I, the undersigned Qualified Person, hereby certify that batch [BATCH NUMBER] of [PRODUCT NAME] is CONDITIONALLY RELEASED with the following explicit condition and Quality Risk Management documentation:*
>
> *EXCEPTIONAL CIRCUMSTANCE: [Specific deviation or investigation conclusion that prompted conditional release]*
>
> *QUALITY RISK MANAGEMENT ASSESSMENT: [Summary of ICH Q9 assessment concluding negligible risk]*
>
> *This conditional release is supported by the full Quality Risk Management documentation attached to this batch dossier. The batch meets all registered specifications. The risk posed by the exceptional circumstance, after mitigation and assessment, is considered negligible for intended use. I accept personal professional responsibility for this conditional release decision."*

## 9. CANNABIS-SPECIFIC BATCH RELEASE CONSIDERATIONS

### 9.1 **Cannabinoid Profile Verification**

Cannabis products contain multiple cannabinoids with varying pharmacological properties. The QP must verify:

#### **THC Content Assessment**

**Specification Limits:**
- EU GMP Annex 16 Requirement 1.7.5 requires all cannabinoid test results to be satisfactory
- Ph.Eur. Monograph 3028 specifies: **Limit of Detection appropriate, resolution >2.0**
- Marketing Authorisation specifies approved THC range (e.g., NMT 22%, or specific therapeutic window)

**Verification Protocol:**
- ✓ Confirm THC measured by HPLC per Ph.Eur. 3028 Cannabinoid identification
- ✓ If THCA present, apply conversion factor: Total THC = THC + (THCA × 0.877)
- ✓ Compare Total THC to registered specification
- ✓ If Total THC exceeds specification: Initiate OOS investigation
- ✓ If Total THC within specification ±10% analytical tolerance: Acceptable
- ✓ Review batch-to-batch cannabinoid trend: Any unexpected variation suggests strain substitution or environmental impact

#### **CBD Content Assessment**

**Specification Limits:**
- For CBD-enriched products: Specify approved range (e.g., CBD 12-18%)
- For THC-dominant products: Confirm CBD within expected minor cannabinoid range
- For balanced products: Confirm CBD/THC ratio within approved window

**Verification Protocol:**
- ✓ Confirm CBD measured by HPLC with same validation as THC
- ✓ If CBDA present: Apply conversion factor: Total CBD = CBD + (CBDA × 0.877)
- ✓ Compare Total CBD to specification
- ✓ Verify that CBD level is consistent with product chemotype (THC-dominant, CBD-dominant, or balanced)

#### **Total Cannabinoid Content**

**Pharmacopeial Requirements:**
- Ph.Eur. 3028 defines: Total cannabinoids = sum of all identified cannabinoids
- Typical range for cannabis flower: 10-25% total cannabinoids (THC + CBD + minor cannabinoids)
- Products with total <5% may indicate immature flower or processing loss

#### **Minor Cannabinoids**

Cannabis products contain minor cannabinoids that may have therapeutic or psychoactive effects:

| Cannabinoid | Typical % | Regulatory Status | QP Assessment |
|------------|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| **CBG** | 0.5–2% | Generally permitted | Confirm within historical range |
| **CBC** | 0.5–1.5% | Generally permitted | Confirm within historical range |
| **CBN** | 0.5–3% | Depends on jurisdiction | Verify acceptable; increasing CBN may indicate aged flower or oxidative degradation |
| **THCV** | 0.5–2% (strain-specific) | Depends on jurisdiction; some markets restrict | Confirm within approved range for strain |
| **CBDV** | <0.5% (strain-specific) | Permitted in most jurisdictions | Confirm presence/absence consistent with strain identity |

### 9.2 **Trichome Maturity Assessment (Supportive, Not Primary)**

**Important Note:** Trichome maturity assessment is **supportive** to cannabinoid analysis, not the primary determination of harvest timing or batch acceptability.

#### **Trichome Color Progression**

| Stage | Color | Microscopic View | Cannabinoid Status | Implication |
|-------|-------|------------------|------------------|-----------|
| **Clear** | Transparent, glass-like | Stalked trichome heads with clear resin | THCA abundant; THC minimal | Flower immature; inadequate potency |
| **Cloudy/Milky** | Opaque, white/milky | Resin heads milky white; light diffusion | THCA converting to THC; some CBN forming | Approaching optimal harvest; suitable potency |
| **Amber** | Golden brown | Resin heads amber-tinted; refraction changes | THCA mostly converted to THC; CBN accumulating | Full maturity; THC at peak; CBN increasing |

#### **Trichome Assessment Procedure**

1. **Sampling:** Collect 3–5 representative flower samples from batch
2. **Microscopy:** Examine under 30–100× magnification
3. **Assessment:** Determine percentage of clear, cloudy, and amber trichomes
4. **Documentation:** Record observations with representative images (optional)
5. **Interpretation:**
   - **Optimal harvest window:** 70% cloudy + 30% amber = peak THC with balanced cannabinoid profile
   - **All clear:** Flower immature; potency suboptimal (would expect low THC measurement)
   - **All amber:** Flower overripe; CBN elevated; THC may be degrading (would expect lower THC, higher CBN)

#### **Trichome Assessment Support for Cannabinoid Results**

The QP uses trichome assessment as **corroborating evidence** of cannabinoid measurement validity:

| Finding | Implication |
|---------|-----------|
| HPLC shows THC 20%, Trichome assessment shows 70% cloudy + 30% amber | ✓ Results are consistent; harvest timing appropriate |
| HPLC shows THC 20%, Trichome assessment shows 90% clear | ⚠️ Inconsistency; flower appears immature but potency is adequate; investigate harvesting decision |
| HPLC shows THC 8%, Trichome assessment shows 100% amber | ⚠️ Inconsistency; flower appears overripe but potency is low; investigate processing loss or strain identification |

### 9.3 **Cultivation-Derived Contamination Risks**

Cannabis cultivation presents specific contamination risks that the QP must assess:

#### **Microbial Contamination**

**Risk Factors:**
- **Substrate:** Soil-based cultivation (outdoor or greenhouse) carries mycobacterium, Clostridium, environmental bacteria
- **Water:** Recirculating irrigation systems can harbor Legionella, Pseudomonas
- **Environment:** Greenhouse humidity (optimal for flowering) creates conditions for microbial proliferation
- **Personnel:** Hand-harvesting introduces skin flora (Staphylococcus aureus, other opportunistic bacteria)

**Testing Requirements (Ph.Eur. 3028):**
- Total Aerobic Microbial Count: ≤10⁴ CFU/g (spectrometry method) or ≤10³ CFU/mL (if extract)
- Enterobacteria: Absent/absent (most stringent standard)
- Candida and other yeasts: ≤10² CFU/g
- Salmonella, Listeria monocytogenes: Absent in 25 g

**QP Assessment:**
- ✓ Confirm all microbial limits met with ±10% tolerance
- ✓ If values approach limits: Assess storage conditions and shelf-life stability
- ✓ If microbial count elevated: Assess cultivation and post-harvest procedures for contamination source

#### **Mycotoxin Contamination**

**Risk Factors:**
- **Botrytis (budrot):** Fungal infection during late flowering in humid conditions; produces aflatoxins and other mycotoxins
- **Environmental fungi:** Aspergillus, Penicillium species naturally present in soil and air
- **Storage:** Post-harvest moisture >12% creates mycotoxin proliferation risk

**Testing Requirements (Regulation 1881/2006):**
- Aflatoxin B1: ≤2 µg/kg (health-based limit)
- Total Aflatoxins (B1+B2+G1+G2): ≤4 µg/kg
- Ochratoxin A: ≤20 µg/kg (tentative limit pending harmonization)
- Other mycotoxins (Fumonisin, Deoxynivalenol): Depending on jurisdiction

**QP Assessment:**
- ✓ Confirm all mycotoxin limits met; aflatoxin B1 <2 µg/kg is mandatory
- ✓ Assess growing conditions and storage; elevated mycotoxins suggest process improvement needed
- ✓ Review cultivation records for evidence of Botrytis management during flowering

#### **Pesticide Residues**

**Risk Factors:**
- Cannabis cultivation commonly uses pest management inputs (insecticides, fungicides, miticides)
- Both legal (registered for cannabis) and potentially unregistered products may be used
- Residue persistence depends on active ingredient, application timing, and environmental breakdown

**Testing Requirements (Regulation 396/2005 / Ph.Eur. 2.8.13):**
- Maximum Residue Limits (MRL) for cannabinoid products still being harmonized
- Common limits for comparable herbal products: <0.1 mg/kg (trace) to 10 mg/kg depending on pesticide
- High-risk pesticides (neonicotinoids, pyrethroids, organophosphates): Often restricted to <0.1 mg/kg

**QP Assessment:**
- ✓ Confirm all identified pesticide residues are below applicable limits
- ✓ Assess IPM documentation to understand pest management approach
- ✓ If residues detected: Verify application timing was adequate for residue breakdown before harvest
- ✓ If residues approaching limits: Assess stability during storage

#### **Heavy Metals Contamination**

**Risk Factors:**
- Cannabis is a **bioaccumulator** for heavy metals
- Soil, water, and atmospheric deposition contribute to metal uptake
- Root uptake is primary mechanism; translocation to flowers is significant

**Testing Requirements (Ph.Eur. 2.4.27 / ICH Q3D):**
- Lead (Pb): ≤2.5 mg/kg (EU safety limit for herbal products)
- Cadmium (Cd): ≤1.0 mg/kg (EU safety limit)
- Mercury (Hg): ≤0.2 mg/kg (ICH Q3D limit)
- Arsenic (As): ≤0.2 mg/kg (ICH Q3D limit; depends on speciation, inorganic As more concerning)

**QP Assessment:**
- ✓ Confirm all heavy metals meet specifications; lead and cadmium are most commonly elevated in cannabis
- ✓ Assess cultivation site soil quality; heavy metal profile may reflect environmental contamination
- ✓ If metals approaching limits: Consider cultivation site assessment and potential interventions

### 9.4 **Strain and Genetic Identity Verification**

The QP must verify that the released batch corresponds to the approved product:

#### **Botanical Identity Verification**

**Methods for Identity Confirmation:**
- **Macro/Microscopic Examination:** Cannabis sativa L. flowers have characteristic morphology
  - Flower bracts covered with trichomes bearing characteristic glandular resin heads
  - Recognizable leaf morphology when present
  - Characteristic inflorescence structure
- **TLC/HPTLC:** Thin-layer chromatography shows characteristic cannabinoid spot pattern (Rf values for THC, CBD, THCA, CBDA)
- **DNA Testing:** Optional, PCR testing of specific cannabis marker genes confirms species identity
- **Cannabinoid Profile:** The specific ratio of THC:CBD is species-consistent and strain-specific

**QP Assessment:**
- ✓ Confirm botanical identity testing documentation
- ✓ Verify macro/microscopic examination shows characteristic cannabis morphology
- ✓ Confirm TLC pattern shows appropriate cannabinoid profile
- ✓ No evidence of foreign plant material
- ✓ Cannabinoid potency consistent with expected strain

#### **Strain and Chemotype Verification**

For cannabis products, multiple strains may be approved (e.g., "Bediol" THC-dominant, "Bedrolite" CBD-dominant). The QP must confirm the correct strain is released:

| Verification Point | Assessment |
|--------------------|-----------|
| **Strain Name** | Batch clearly identified as approved strain (e.g., "Bediol," "Bedrolite") |
| **Chemotype** | THC/CBD ratio consistent with approved chemotype (THC-dominant, CBD-dominant, or balanced) |
| **Potency Range** | THC and CBD content within strain-specific specification window |
| **Minor Cannabinoid Profile** | Presence/absence of CBG, THCV, etc. consistent with strain identity |
| **Cannabinoid Trend** | Batch potency consistent with previous batches of same strain (no unexpected drift) |

### 9.5 **Shelf-Life Stability and Expiry Dating**

Cannabis products degrade during storage; the QP must verify expiry dating is justified:

#### **Cannabinoid Degradation During Storage**

**Primary Degradation Pathway:**
- THC → CBN (oxidative degradation, temperature and light-accelerated)
- THCA → THC (decarboxylation, temperature and light-accelerated)
- CBD → CBL (cyclization under acidic conditions)

**Degradation Rate Factors:**
- **Temperature:** 25°C vs. 4°C produces ~2-fold difference in THC degradation rate
- **Light:** UV and visible light significantly accelerate THC → CBN conversion
- **Humidity:** <10% or >12% moisture can affect degradation rates
- **Packaging:** Opaque, moisture-barrier packaging (e.g., amber glass, aluminum foil) slows degradation

**Specification Acceptance During Shelf-Life:**
- Batch must remain within registered specification (e.g., THC NMT 22%) for entire claimed shelf-life
- Worst-case storage conditions (e.g., 25°C, ambient light) should be considered in expiry assignment
- Accelerated stability data (e.g., 40°C/75% RH for 6 months) supports longer shelf-lives

#### **QP Verification of Expiry Dating**

The QP confirms:
- ✓ Stability programme data available and current
- ✓ Batch potency at release is within specification
- ✓ Worst-case degradation rate documented
- ✓ Proposed expiry date supported by stability data
- ✓ Storage conditions on packaging match those used in stability study (e.g., "Store below 25°C, avoid light")

## 10. HANDLING UNEXPECTED DEVIATIONS

### 10.1 **Framework for Deviations During Batch Cycle**

EU GMP Annex 16, Section 3 specifically addresses deviations that occur during the batch cycle:

> *"Where in exceptional cases and only for the final product release, a batch has not fully complied with one or more of the requirements of the Manufacturing Authorisation, the person responsible for releasing the batch can nevertheless, on the basis of a thorough assessment of the deviation and of the results of analysis of the batch in question and of any other batch which might be affected, release the batch if the breach is not such as to result in any risk to the safety of the patients."*

### 10.2 **Conditions for Certification Despite Deviation**

**All five of the following conditions must be SIMULTANEOUSLY satisfied** before the QP may certify a batch with a deviation:

#### **Condition 1: Batch Meets All Specifications**

- ✓ **No out-of-specification results:** All quality control testing results are within registered specifications
- ✓ **Specification compliance confirmed:** CoA shows all tests passed
- ✓ If deviation caused result to approach specification limit, re-testing confirms compliance
- **Cannot certify:** Any OOS result, regardless of investigation findings, precludes certification

#### **Condition 2: Investigation is Complete**

- ✓ **Root cause identified:** Deviation investigation has determined the cause
- ✓ **Timeline documented:** When deviation was discovered, what was the investigation sequence
- ✓ **All relevant data collected:** Manufacturing parameters, environmental data, personnel interviews completed
- ✓ **Conclusions documented:** Formal investigation report signed by Head of Production or QC
- **Cannot certify:** If investigation is ongoing or inconclusive; batch must be held pending investigation completion

#### **Condition 3: Quality Risk Management Assessment Concludes Negligible Risk**

- ✓ **Formal ICH Q9 QRM assessment conducted:** Using systematic methodology (failure mode analysis, risk matrix, or equivalent)
- ✓ **Risk assessment documented:** Specific identification of potential patient safety impact
- ✓ **Mitigation measures implemented:** If any risk was identified, corrective action confirms mitigation
- ✓ **Residual risk assessed as negligible:** After mitigation, risk is determined to be insignificant
- ✓ **QRM documentation attached to batch record:** Assessor signs QRM report confirming conclusions

**Example QRM Assessment:**
> *Deviation: Manufacturing temperature exceeded 22°C for 4 hours during flowering Phase 8*
> *Risk Assessment: Excessive temperature during flowering may affect trichome resin development and cannabinoid synthesis*
> *Severity: Moderate; Occurrence: Single instance; Detection: Immediate (4-hour window)*
> *Batch Testing Results: THC measured 20.5% (within specification 20–22%), cannabinoid profile normal*
> *Mitigation: Temperature deviation corrected immediately; HVAC system maintenance completed; future reoccurrence prevented*
> *Residual Risk Assessment: Negligible — batch meets all specifications; cannabinoid profile normal; similar deviations in past batches showed no quality impact*
> *QRM Conclusion: Risk to patient safety is negligible; batch suitable for release*

#### **Condition 4: Regulatory Requirement Compliance**

- ✓ **Competent authority consultation (if applicable):** For significant deviations, consider regulatory notification
- ✓ **Marketing Authorisation Variation (if needed):** If deviation represents a permanent process change, variation may be required to MALMED before release
- ✓ **Safety update (if needed):** If deviation relates to safety issue, assessment whether Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR) update is triggered
- **Cannot certify:** If regulatory requirement indicates batch must not be released

#### **Condition 5: Corrective Action Prevents Recurrence**

- ✓ **Root cause correction implemented:** The identified cause of deviation has been corrected
- ✓ **Preventive action documented:** Formal CAPA assigned, target completion date set
- ✓ **System improvement confirmed:** Quality system has been enhanced to prevent similar deviation in future batches
- **Cannot certify:** If root cause has not been addressed; batch must be held pending corrective action

### 10.3 **Documentation Required for Deviation Certification**

When the QP certifies a batch that includes a deviation, the batch record must include:

1. **Original Deviation Report** — Dated, signed, describing deviation in detail
2. **Deviation Investigation Report** — Root cause analysis, corrective actions, effectiveness verification
3. **Quality Risk Management Assessment** — ICH Q9 assessment concluding negligible patient risk
4. **CAPA Record** — Corrective/preventive actions assigned, target dates, assignee
5. **QA Recommendation Statement** — Explicit recommendation regarding certification despite deviation
6. **QP Certification Statement** — **Modified statement** explicitly addressing the deviation:

**Modified QP Certification Statement for Deviations:**

> *"I, the undersigned Qualified Person, hereby certify that batch [BATCH NUMBER] of [PRODUCT NAME] is CERTIFIED FOR RELEASE despite the following documented deviation.*
>
> *DEVIATION: [Specific description of deviation, e.g., "Environmental humidity exceeded 55% for 6 hours during Week 8 of flowering"]*
>
> *BASIS FOR CERTIFICATION:*
> *1. The batch meets all registered specifications: [THC 20.5% (spec 20–22%), all other tests within limits]*
> *2. The deviation has been fully investigated; root cause [description] has been identified and corrected*
> *3. Formal Quality Risk Management assessment per ICH Q9 concludes the risk to patient safety is negligible*
> *4. Corrective action has been implemented to prevent recurrence: [description]*
> *5. No regulatory constraint precludes certification*
>
> *The batch is therefore suitable for release, subject to the documented Quality Risk Management justification included in the batch dossier. All Annex 16 requirements have been satisfied."*

### 10.4 **Batches That Cannot Be Certified**

**The QP CANNOT certify a batch if:**

- ❌ Any OOS result exists (regardless of investigation findings)
- ❌ Specification compliance is uncertain or borderline (within analytical tolerance of limit)
- ❌ Investigation remains incomplete
- ❌ QRM assessment cannot conclude negligible risk
- ❌ Significant GMP violation is identified (e.g., equipment not qualified, procedure not followed, cleanroom breach)
- ❌ Data integrity concerns exist (evidence of falsification, unexplained corrections, missing signatures)
- ❌ Supply chain cannot be verified
- ❌ Regulatory constraint prohibits release
- ❌ Required information is unavailable or irretrievable

**In these cases, the batch must be REJECTED.**

All batch release documentation must comply with ALCOA+ principles:

- **Legible:** All handwriting clear and readable; no coffee stains or illegibility
- **Contemporaneous:** Data recorded at time of activity; not back-filled
- **Original:** Original documents or certified true copies
- **Accurate:** Data correctly represents actual event
- **Complete:** No missing entries
- **Consistent:** Same batch number, dates throughout
- **Enduring:** Records remain in readable condition
