## 1. PURPOSE

1.1 This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the requirements for vegetative propagation (cloning) of cannabis plants at <span class="var">[FACILITY_NAME]</span>.

1.2 The procedure ensures:
- Consistent production of genetically identical plants from mother stock
- High rooting success rates (<span class="var">[≥90%]</span>)
- Traceability from mother plant through propagation to production
- Prevention of contamination and disease transmission
- Compliance with EU GMP and GACP requirements

---

## 2. SCOPE

2.1 This SOP applies to:
- Cutting preparation and treatment
- Rooting hormone application
- Propagation environment management
- Root development monitoring
- Hardening and transplant readiness
- Batch documentation and traceability

2.2 This SOP does **not** cover:
- Mother plant management (see <span class="var">[PRO_01.01]</span>)
- Seed germination (see <span class="var">[PRO_01.03]</span>)
- Post-transplant vegetative growth (see <span class="var">[PRO_01.04]</span>)

## 4. DEFINITIONS

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Clone** | A genetically identical plant produced through vegetative propagation |
| **Cutting** | A section of plant material removed from a mother plant for propagation |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Chemical compound (auxin-based) that stimulates root development |
| **Propagation Dome** | Humidity-controlled enclosure for rooting cuttings |
| **Callus** | Undifferentiated plant tissue that forms at cut site before roots |
| **Root Initials** | First visible root structures emerging from cutting |
| **Hardening Off** | Process of gradually acclimating rooted cuttings to lower humidity |
| **Propagation Batch** | Group of cuttings taken and processed together with common documentation |

## 8. PROCEDURE

### 8.1 Pre-Propagation Preparation

#### 8.1.1 Batch Initiation

1. Create Propagation Batch Record <span class="var">[FM-PROP-001]</span>
2. Assign Batch Number: <span class="var">[PROP-YYYYMMDD-###]</span>
3. Record:
   - Planned quantity
   - Target cultivar(s)
   - Mother plant source(s)
   - Scheduled dates
4. Obtain supervisor authorization

#### 8.1.2 Area Preparation

1. Clean propagation area per <span class="var">[PRO_05.01]</span>
2. Sanitize all surfaces with <span class="var">[approved disinfectant]</span>
3. Verify environmental controls operational
4. Check calibration status of meters

#### 8.1.3 Material Preparation

**Rooting Cube Preparation:**

1. Calculate required cubes:
   - Planned cuttings + <span class="var">[10%]</span> contingency

2. Pre-soak rooting cubes:
   - Prepare pH-adjusted water: <span class="var">[pH 5.5]</span>
   - Submerge cubes for <span class="var">[30-60 minutes]</span>
   - Allow to drain for <span class="var">[5-10 minutes]</span>
   - Target moisture: saturated but not dripping

3. Arrange in clone trays:
   - Position cubes in tray cells
   - Ensure proper spacing
   - Label tray with batch information

**Tool Preparation:**

1. Select cutting instruments:
   - Scalpels or razor blades
   - Sterilize in <span class="var">[70% IPA]</span> for <span class="var">[30 seconds]</span>
   - Allow to air dry or flame sterilize

2. Prepare rooting hormone:
   - Use fresh hormone gel (check expiry)
   - Dispense small amount to secondary container
   - NEVER dip cuttings directly into master container (contamination risk)

### 8.2 Cutting Harvest and Preparation

#### 8.2.1 Cutting Selection Criteria

Select cuttings from mother plants that meet:

| Criterion | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Mother plant status | Healthy, no pests/disease, vegetative |
| Cutting length | <span class="var">[10-15 cm]</span> |
| Node count | <span class="var">[3-4 nodes minimum]</span> |
| Stem diameter | <span class="var">[3-5 mm]</span> |
| Stem condition | Semi-hardwood (not too soft or woody) |
| Leaf condition | Healthy, no damage or deficiency |

#### 8.2.2 Cutting Procedure

**Step 1: Initial Cut from Mother**

1. Identify suitable cutting site on mother plant
2. Make cut at <span class="var">[45° angle]</span> using sterile blade
3. Cut <span class="var">[1-2 cm below]</span> a node
4. Immediately place cut end in water
5. Record cutting harvest in <span class="var">[FM-CULT-008]</span>

**Step 2: Cutting Preparation**

1. Work at clean preparation station
2. For each cutting:

   a. **Trim to final length:**
   - Target: <span class="var">[10-12 cm]</span>
   - Make fresh 45° cut just below lowest node
   
   b. **Remove lower leaves:**
   - Remove leaves from bottom <span class="var">[1-2 nodes]</span>
   - Leave <span class="var">[2-3 leaf sets]</span> at top
   
   c. **Trim large leaves (optional):**
   - Cut large fan leaves in half
   - Reduces transpiration during rooting
   
   d. **Scoring (optional for woody stems):**
   - Make <span class="var">[2-3 vertical scratches, 1 cm long]</span>
   - On lower stem to expose cambium

3. Keep prepared cuttings in water until hormone application

#### 8.2.3 Rooting Hormone Application

1. Remove cutting from water
2. Gently shake off excess water
3. Dip cut end into rooting hormone gel:
   - Depth: <span class="var">[1-2 cm]</span>
   - Duration: <span class="var">[3-5 seconds]</span>
4. Ensure stem end is fully coated
5. Do NOT remove excess hormone

### 8.3 Planting and Initial Care

#### 8.3.1 Inserting Cuttings

1. Create hole in rooting cube:
   - Use dibble or clean implement
   - Diameter slightly larger than stem
   - Depth: <span class="var">[2-3 cm]</span>

2. Insert cutting:
   - Gently place hormone-coated stem into hole
   - Do NOT push - avoid wiping off hormone
   - Cutting should stand upright

3. Firm cube around stem:
   - Gently press cube to ensure contact
   - Cutting should be stable

4. Label immediately:
   - Mother plant ID
   - Cultivar
   - Date
   - Position reference (if applicable)

#### 8.3.2 Dome Setup

1. Place planted tray under dome
2. Add <span class="var">[1-2 cm]</span> water to bottom of tray (for humidity)
3. Close dome vents completely (Day 0)
4. Position under lights:
   - Height for <span class="var">[200 PPFD]</span>
   - Timer set for <span class="var">[18/6 photoperiod]</span>

5. Activate heating mat if used:
   - Set to <span class="var">[25°C]</span>
   - Verify temperature with probe

6. Record in batch record:
   - Time of planting
   - Number planted
   - Dome conditions
   - Operator initials

#### 8.3.3 First 72 Hours (Critical Period)

**Day 0-3 Protocol:**

| Hour | Action |
|------|--------|
| 0-4 | Mist dome interior if humidity <90% |
| 12 | Check humidity, mist if needed |
| 24 | Visual inspection, remove any dead cuttings |
| 36 | Check for wilting, ensure adequate humidity |
| 48 | Brief dome opening (<30 sec) for air exchange |
| 72 | First vent opening if no wilting |

**Critical Monitoring:**

- Humidity must remain <span class="var">[>90%]</span>
- No direct sunlight or high-intensity light
- No drafts or temperature fluctuations
- Remove any cuttings showing disease immediately

### 8.4 Rooting Phase Management

#### 8.4.1 Daily Monitoring (Day 3-14)

Perform daily at consistent time:

| Check | Action | Record |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Dome humidity | Maintain <span class="var">[85-95%]</span> | Log reading |
| Temperature | Verify within range | Log reading |
| Cutting condition | Assess wilting, discoloration | Note abnormalities |
| Disease signs | Look for mold, rot, yellowing | Remove affected, report |
| Moisture level | Check cube moisture | Mist if dry |

#### 8.4.2 Progressive Hardening Schedule

| Day | Vent Status | Target Humidity | Duration |
|-----|-------------|-----------------|----------|
| 0-3 | Closed | <span class="var">[95%+]</span> | Full day |
| 4-5 | <span class="var">[1/4 open]</span> | <span class="var">[90%]</span> | Full day |
| 6-7 | <span class="var">[1/2 open]</span> | <span class="var">[85%]</span> | Full day |
| 8-10 | <span class="var">[3/4 open]</span> | <span class="var">[80%]</span> | Full day |
| 11-14 | <span class="var">[Full open / dome off]</span> | <span class="var">[70-75%]</span> | Monitor closely |

**Adjust schedule based on:**
- Cutting response (wilting = slow down)
- Ambient room humidity
- Root development progress

#### 8.4.3 Root Development Assessment

**Root Development Stages:**

| Stage | Description | Typical Day | Action |
|-------|-------------|-------------|--------|
| 1. Callus formation | White tissue at cut site | Day <span class="var">[3-5]</span> | Continue protocol |
| 2. Root initials | Tiny white bumps visible | Day <span class="var">[5-7]</span> | Begin hardening |
| 3. Root emergence | Roots <span class="var">[1-3 cm]</span> | Day <span class="var">[7-10]</span> | Accelerate hardening |
| 4. Root establishment | Roots <span class="var">[3-5 cm, branching]</span> | Day <span class="var">[10-14]</span> | Ready for transplant |

**Inspection Method:**
- Gently lift cube from tray
- Observe bottom and sides for root emergence
- Do NOT pull cutting from cube
- Return carefully to same position

### 8.5 Foliar Feeding (Optional)

If cuttings show signs of nutrient need (yellowing) after Day 7:

1. Prepare dilute nutrient solution:
   - <span class="var">[1/4 strength]</span> vegetative nutrients
   - pH adjusted to <span class="var">[6.0]</span>
   - EC: <span class="var">[0.3-0.5 mS/cm]</span>

2. Foliar application:
   - Fine mist sprayer
   - Apply to undersides of leaves
   - Early morning or evening (lights off)
   - Avoid saturating cubes

3. Record application in batch record

### 8.6 Transplant Readiness Assessment

#### 8.6.1 Readiness Criteria

Cuttings are ready for transplant when:

| Criterion | Specification | Method |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| Root length | <span class="var">[≥3 cm]</span> | Visual inspection |
| Root density | Multiple roots visible | Visual inspection |
| Root color | White, healthy | Visual inspection |
| Dome tolerance | 24 hours without dome, no wilt | Observation |
| New growth | Visible new leaf development | Visual inspection |
| Days from cutting | <span class="var">[10-14 days minimum]</span> | Record |

#### 8.6.2 Final Quality Assessment

Before release to vegetative:

1. Visual inspection of each clone:
   - Root development adequate
   - No pest or disease signs
   - Healthy foliage color
   - Structural integrity

2. Grade each clone:
   
   | Grade | Criteria | Action |
   |-------|----------|--------|
   | A | Excellent roots, vigorous growth | Release to veg |
   | B | Adequate roots, acceptable growth | Release to veg |
   | C | Marginal roots, weak growth | Hold for recovery or cull |
   | Reject | Poor roots, disease, severe issues | Cull and dispose |

3. Record in Propagation Batch Record:
   - Total planted
   - Total rooted
   - Grade distribution
   - Rejects and reasons
   - Rooting success rate: <span class="var">[Target ≥90%]</span>

### 8.7 Transfer to Vegetative

#### 8.7.1 Transfer Procedure

1. Complete Propagation Batch Completion section
2. Generate clone labels for transfer:
   - Batch number
   - Cultivar
   - Clone number within batch
   - Mother plant source

3. Coordinate with Vegetative Room:
   - Confirm readiness to receive
   - Verify environmental conditions
   - Prepare receiving containers

4. Physical transfer:
   - Transport in covered trays
   - Minimize environmental stress
   - Complete transfer documentation

#### 8.7.2 Handover Documentation

Complete transfer form <span class="var">[FM-PROP-003]</span>:
- Batch ID
- Quantity transferred
- Quality grade summary
- Receiving room/location
- Receiving operator signature
- Transfer date/time

### 8.8 Failed Cutting Management

#### 8.8.1 Early Failure (Day 0-5)

If cutting fails within first 5 days:
- Remove from tray immediately
- Assess cause (wilting, rot, contamination)
- Document in batch record
- May replace from same-day cuts only
- Clean position before reuse

#### 8.8.2 Late Failure (Day 6+)

If cutting fails after Day 5:
- Remove and document
- Inspect adjacent cuttings
- If pattern suggests disease → isolate tray
- No replacement - affects batch metrics

#### 8.8.3 Batch Failure Investigation

If rooting rate falls below <span class="var">[80%]</span>:
1. Initiate deviation report
2. Investigate potential causes:
   - Mother plant health
   - Environmental conditions
   - Technique consistency
   - Material quality (hormone, cubes)
3. Implement corrective actions
4. Document investigation and CAPA
