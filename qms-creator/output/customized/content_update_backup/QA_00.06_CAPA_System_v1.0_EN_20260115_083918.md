## 1. PURPOSE

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes a systematic framework for **identifying, investigating, and resolving quality issues** through **Corrective Actions (CAs)** and **Preventive Actions (PAs)** at Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility. The CAPA system ensures that:

- **Root causes** of nonconformances and deviations are thoroughly identified and analyzed
- **Corrective actions** eliminate identified problems and prevent recurrence
- **Preventive actions** eliminate potential problems before they occur
- **Systemic issues** are identified and addressed through trending analysis
- **Effectiveness** of all actions is verified and sustained over time
- **Regulatory compliance** with EU GMP (Annex 7), ICH Q9 (Quality Risk Management), and ICH Q10 (Pharmaceutical Quality System) is maintained

The CAPA system is a critical component of the quality management system and directly supports Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility's commitment to continuous improvement and product quality assurance.

## 2. SCOPE

### 2.1 Applicability

This SOP applies to **all CAPA activities** at Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility, including:

- **Quality Issues**: Deviations, nonconformances, out-of-specification results, customer complaints
- **Regulatory Issues**: Regulatory observations, inspection findings, regulatory non-compliance
- **Process Issues**: Process upsets, equipment failures, unexpected variability
- **Personnel Issues**: Personnel errors, training gaps, competency concerns
- **Systemic Issues**: Systemic deficiencies identified through trending or management review
- **Preventive Actions**: Anticipated risks and potential quality issues identified proactively

### 2.2 Exclusions

The following are **NOT managed as CAPAs** but through their respective systems:

- **Document Changes**: Managed under QA_00.02_v1 Document Control SOP
- **Facility Changes**: Managed under QA_00.05_v1 Change Control SOP
- **Supplier Issues**: Managed under MAT-00-004 Supplier Management SOP
- **Personnel Training**: Managed under HRM-00-001 Personnel Management SOP
- **Equipment Maintenance**: Managed under EQU-00-001 Equipment Management SOP

However, if these issues are **recurring or systemic**, a CAPA investigation may be initiated to address root causes.

## 3. RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

### 3.1 Quality Manager (Quality Assurance Manager)

- **Initiates CAPAs** when quality issues are identified or escalated
- **Assigns investigation teams** with appropriate subject matter experts
- **Ensures compliance** with CAPA timelines and procedures
- **Reviews and approves** root cause analyses and corrective/preventive action plans
- **Monitors CAPA status** through management review meetings
- **Escalates** high-risk or overdue CAPAs to Quality Manager or QP
- **Authorizes CAPA closure** only after effectiveness is confirmed

### 3.2 Quality Manager

- **Reviews** all high-risk CAPAs and effectiveness reviews
- **Approves** CAPAs involving regulatory implications or critical process changes
- **Resolves conflicts** between departments on appropriate corrective actions
- **Ensures** CAPA trending is presented in management review
- **Recommends** systemic improvements based on CAPA trends

### 3.3 Quality Assurance Manager

- **Coordinates CAPA investigations** with multiple departments
- **Schedules** root cause analysis meetings and effectiveness review meetings
- **Maintains CAPA register** and tracking documentation
- **Generates** CAPA reports and effectiveness summaries
- **Alerts** responsible parties of approaching action deadlines

### 3.4 Department Managers / Process Owners

- **Implement approved corrective and preventive actions** within assigned timelines
- **Provide technical expertise** during root cause analysis meetings
- **Document completion** of all action items with evidence
- **Report barriers** to implementation immediately to Quality Manager
- **Participate** in effectiveness review meetings
- **Identify root causes** within their areas of responsibility

### 3.5 Subject Matter Experts (SMEs)

- **Participate** in root cause analysis meetings and investigations
- **Provide specialized knowledge** on technical aspects of the issue
- **Recommend** appropriate corrective and preventive actions
- **Verify completion** of technical action items
- **Contribute** to effectiveness verification

### 3.6 All Personnel

- **Report** observed quality issues or deviations immediately
- **Participate** in effectiveness verification activities within their roles
- **Comply** with new or modified procedures resulting from CAPAs
- **Provide feedback** on CAPA implementation effectiveness

## 4. DEFINITIONS

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **CAPA** | Corrective and Preventive Action - systematic process for identifying, investigating, and resolving quality issues |
| **Corrective Action (CA)** | Action taken to eliminate an identified cause of a nonconformance or defect to prevent recurrence |
| **Preventive Action (PA)** | Action taken to eliminate a potential cause of a nonconformance before it occurs |
| **Nonconformance** | Failure to meet specified requirements; includes deviations, OOS results, customer complaints, audit findings |
| **Root Cause** | The fundamental reason why a problem occurred; the original cause that, if eliminated, would prevent recurrence |
| **Root Cause Analysis (RCA)** | Systematic investigation technique to identify the root cause(s) of an issue using structured methodologies |

## 5. PROCEDURE

### 5.1 CAPA Initiation

#### 5.1.1 Triggering Events

A CAPA **must be initiated** when any of the following occur:

**Quality Issues**:
- ☐ Out-of-Specification (OOS) or Out-of-Limits (OOL) test results
- ☐ Product nonconformance or quality attribute excursion
- ☐ Deviation from established procedures or work instructions
- ☐ Customer complaint regarding product quality or batch record

**Operational Issues**:
- ☐ Equipment failure or unexpected malfunction
- ☐ Process upset or deviation from established parameters
- ☐ Unexpected batch failure or yield loss (>10% variance)
- ☐ Environmental monitoring excursion (temperature, humidity, microbial)

**Regulatory Issues**:
- ☐ Regulatory inspection observation or warning letter
- ☐ Non-compliance with GMP requirements
- ☐ Regulatory agency inquiry or requirement
- ☐ Internal audit finding requiring root cause analysis

**Systemic/Preventive Issues**:
- ☐ Recurring problem identified through trending (3+ occurrences in 12 months)
- ☐ Potential risk identified through risk assessment or management review
- ☐ Effectiveness verification failure of previous CAPA
- ☐ Personnel training gap or competency concern

#### 5.1.2 CAPA Initiation Process

**Step 1: Issue Documentation**
- Issue is documented in deviation report, deviation log, or complaint record
- Quality Manager reviews issue for CAPA requirement
- If CAPA required → proceed to CAPAN assignment

**Step 2: CAPAN Assignment**
- Quality Assurance Manager assigns unique CAPA Number (CAPAN-YYYY-####)
- CAPA Request Form (Annex A01) completed with:
  - Triggering event description
  - Product(s) or process(es) affected
  - Severity assessment (Low/Medium/High/Critical)
  - Preliminary impact assessment
  - Proposed timeline for investigation
- Date initiated recorded

**Step 3: Investigation Team Assignment**
- Quality Manager identifies investigation team composition:
  - **Team Leader**: Typically QA Manager or subject matter expert
  - **Core Members**: Department manager, technical specialist, QA representative
  - **Extended Members**: Additional SMEs as needed for complex investigations
- Team composition documented in CAPA Request Form

**Step 4: Initial Review and Planning**
- Investigation team meets within **3 working days** of CAPA initiation
- Establish investigation timeline:
  - **Low-risk CAPAs**: Root cause analysis within 2 weeks
  - **Medium-risk CAPAs**: Root cause analysis within 10 working days
  - **High-risk CAPAs**: Root cause analysis within 5 working days
  - **Critical CAPAs**: Root cause analysis within 2 working days
- Assign investigation responsibilities
- Identify information and data sources needed

### 5.2 Root Cause Analysis (RCA)

#### 5.2.1 RCA Overview

Root Cause Analysis is a **systematic investigation** to determine the fundamental reason(s) why a problem occurred. The goal is to identify the **original cause** that, if eliminated, would prevent recurrence.

**Key Principle**: Addressing symptoms (e.g., "product failed test") without addressing root causes (e.g., "analytical equipment not calibrated") leads to recurring problems.

#### 5.2.2 RCA Methodologies

Five structured RCA methodologies are available for use depending on issue complexity:

**Method 1: 5 Whys Analysis**

**Best for**: Simple issues with single or few root causes

**Procedure**:
1. State the problem clearly: "Test result was OOS for [parameter]"
2. Ask "Why?" and answer: "Analytical method was not calibrated"
3. Ask "Why?" again: "Calibration reminder was not issued"
4. Continue asking "Why?": "Calibration schedule not entered in equipment logbook"
5. Keep asking until root cause is identified: "No procedure for scheduling routine calibration activities"
6. Identify systemic fix: "Implement automated calibration scheduling system"

**Documentation**: Use 5 Whys Worksheet (Annex A02)

**Method 2: Fishbone Diagram (Ishikawa Diagram)**

**Best for**: Complex issues with multiple contributing factors

**Procedure**:
1. State problem in "head" of fishbone: "Batch yield 8% below specification"
2. Categorize potential causes into main branches:
   - **People**: Personnel competency, training, communication
   - **Process**: Procedure clarity, critical parameters, controls
   - **Plant** (Equipment): Equipment performance, condition, maintenance
   - **Materials**: Material quality, supplier performance, specifications
   - **Methods**: Analytical methods, sampling, data recording
   - **Environment**: Temperature, humidity, contamination, utilities
3. Identify specific causes in each category
4. Trace backward: Which factors contributed to the outcome?
5. Identify root causes: Which factors would, if controlled, prevent recurrence?

**Documentation**: Use Fishbone Diagram Template (Annex A02)

**Method 3: Statistical Analysis**

**Best for**: Process-related issues with quantifiable data

**Procedure**:
1. Collect historical data: Test results, batch records, environmental monitoring
2. Organize data chronologically and by variable
3. Identify trend or pattern:
   - Upward/downward trend over time?
   - Correlation with specific equipment or personnel?
   - Correlation with material lot or supplier?
   - Correlation with season, time of day, or shift?
4. Stratify data by potential root cause:
   - By equipment used
   - By personnel or shift
   - By material supplier or lot
   - By time period or environmental conditions
5. Analyze: Does one factor explain the variation?
6. Validate: Is there sufficient data to confirm the relationship?

**Documentation**: Use Statistical Analysis Worksheet (Annex A02)

**Method 4: Brainstorming**

**Best for**: Issues where cause is uncertain and team expertise is valuable

**Procedure**:
1. Assemble cross-functional investigation team
2. Facilitate open brainstorming session (45-60 minutes)
3. Ground rules:
   - All ideas are welcome (no judgment initially)
   - Focus on quantity of ideas, not quality
   - No criticism or debate during brainstorming
   - One idea per person, rotating
4. Record all potential causes on whiteboard or paper
5. Group similar ideas
6. After brainstorming, evaluate each potential cause:
   - Is it factually supported?
   - What evidence would support/disprove it?
   - Can it explain the observed problem?
7. Identify most likely root causes for further investigation

**Documentation**: Use Brainstorming Session Format (Annex A02)

**Method 5: Fault Tree Analysis (FTA)**

**Best for**: Complex systems with multiple failure pathways

**Procedure**:
1. Define top event (the problem): "Batch released with microbial limit exceeding specification"
2. Identify immediate causes (what directly caused this event?):
   - Environmental microorganism contamination
   - Materials source contamination
   - Equipment contamination
   - Personnel error in sampling/testing
3. For each immediate cause, identify its causes:
   - Environmental: Air handling system failure? Environmental monitoring lapsed?
   - Materials: Supplier problem? Material storage issue?
   - Equipment: Cleaning failure? Equipment contamination?
   - Personnel: Training gap? Procedure violation?
4. Continue decomposing until basic events (root causes) identified
5. Identify control points: Which failures could have been prevented by existing controls?
6. Identify gaps: Which failures were not prevented by existing controls?

**Documentation**: Use Fault Tree Analysis Template (Annex A02)

#### 5.2.3 Conducting the RCA Meeting

**Meeting Preparation**:
- Schedule meeting within established timeline (see 5.1.2, Step 4)
- Distribute all relevant documents at least **2 working days before meeting**:
  - Deviation report or complaint record
  - Batch record or process data
  - Previous related CAPAs (if any)
  - Environmental or equipment data
- Prepare RCA worksheets (5 Whys, Fishbone, Statistical Analysis, Brainstorming, or FTA as applicable)

**Meeting Conduct** (Typical duration: 2-4 hours, may require multiple sessions):
1. **Opening** (10 min): Review triggering event, meeting objectives, expected outcome
2. **Data Review** (20-30 min): Present all available facts, data, test results, batch records
3. **RCA Method Selection** (10 min): Team decides which method(s) to use
4. **Root Cause Analysis** (60-120 min): Team systematically analyzes using selected method(s)
5. **Root Cause Validation** (20-30 min): For each identified root cause, ask:
   - "Would eliminating this cause prevent the problem?"
   - "Do we have evidence supporting this root cause?"
   - "Can this be verified through testing, audit, or investigation?"
6. **Systemic Issue Identification** (10 min): Evaluate:
   - "Is this a recurring problem?"
   - "Does it affect multiple areas?"
   - "Are there other similar products/processes at risk?"
7. **Documentation** (10 min): Record RCA findings, root causes identified, systemic issues noted

**Output of RCA Meeting**:
- Root Cause Analysis Report (completed template from Annex A02)
- Identified root causes (minimum 1, typically 2-4)
- Systemic issues identified (if any)
- Preliminary recommendations for corrective/preventive actions

#### 5.2.4 RCA Quality Standards

The RCA **must demonstrate**:

✓ **Factual**: Based on evidence and data, not speculation
✓ **Complete**: Addresses all aspects of the problem, not just symptoms
✓ **Systemic**: Identifies underlying organizational causes, not just individual errors
✓ **Causal**: Explains WHY the problem occurred, not just WHAT happened
✓ **Preventable**: Identifies factors that could have prevented the problem
✓ **Logical**: Clear reasoning from evidence to conclusions

**Quality Check**: If RCA uses words like "user error," "operator mistake," or "oversight" without identifying WHY the error occurred (training gap? procedure unclear? system design?), the RCA is **INCOMPLETE** and requires further analysis.

### 5.3 Corrective Action (CA) Development and Implementation

#### 5.3.1 Corrective Action Definition

A **Corrective Action (CA)** is a specific action taken to:
- **Eliminate the root cause** of a nonconformance or defect
- **Prevent recurrence** of the same problem
- **Restore compliance** with established requirements

CAs are **reactive** - they address problems that have already occurred.

#### 5.3.2 CA Development Process

**Step 1: Root Cause to Action Mapping**

For each identified root cause, develop corrective action(s):

| Root Cause | Corrective Action | Responsible | Timeline | Success Criteria |
|------------|-------------------|-------------|----------|------------------|
| Analytical equipment calibration not performed on schedule | Implement automated calibration reminder system in Laboratory Information System (LIS) | QC Manager | 30 days | Calibration reminder issued automatically 5 days before calibration due date; evidence: LIS configuration, reminder records |
| Personnel not aware of calibration requirement | Conduct training for all QC personnel on calibration procedures (QCS-00-003) | QA Manager | 10 days | Training record completed, competency assessment passing score achieved |
| Procedure does not explicitly require equipment validation before use | Revise QCS-00-003 Section 5.2.1 to state: "Operator must verify equipment is within calibration before starting analysis" | QC Manager | 5 days | Revised SOP approved and documented under QA_00.05_v1 Change Control |

**Step 2: Action Specificity**

Each corrective action **must be specific and measurable**:

**❌ Not Acceptable**: "Improve communication"
**✅ Acceptable**: "Implement daily 15-minute team huddle each morning at 08:00 with QC personnel to review daily testing schedule, equipment status, and open CAPAs"

**❌ Not Acceptable**: "Better training"
**✅ Acceptable**: "Conduct classroom training for Production Team A on Cleaning SOP (SAN-00-001) per Training Protocol HRM-00-005, followed by hands-on competency assessment; training completion by January 31, 2024"

**Step 3: Responsibility and Timeline Assignment**

Each corrective action is assigned to:
- **Responsible Person**: Department Manager or designated individual with authority to implement
- **Target Completion Date**: Based on action criticality and complexity
  - High-risk actions: 5-15 working days
  - Medium-risk actions: 15-30 days
  - Low-risk actions: 30-90 days

#### 5.3.3 Implementation Monitoring

During implementation:

- **Weekly Status Updates** for high-risk CAPAs:
  - Responsible person provides status update to QA Manager every Friday
  - Status: GREEN (on track), YELLOW (minor delay), RED (blocked/delayed)
  - If YELLOW/RED: Identify barrier and corrective action for barrier

- **Mid-timeline Check-in** for all CAPAs:
  - At 50% of planned timeline, verify progress
  - If behind schedule: Escalate to Quality Manager

- **Implementation Barriers**: If implementation is blocked:
  - Report immediately to Quality Manager
  - Propose alternative approach or timeline extension
  - Document barrier and resolution in CAPA register

#### 5.3.4 Implementation Evidence

Upon completion of corrective action, provide **objective evidence**:

- **Training**: Completed training records with assessment scores, attendance sheets, signed-off competency statements
- **Procedure Update**: Approved revised SOP with signatures and effective date documented
- **Equipment Maintenance**: Equipment service records, calibration certificates, maintenance logbooks
- **System Configuration**: Screenshots of system settings, logbook entries, testing results after change
- **Physical Improvement**: Inspection records, photographs, compliance certifications

**Evidence Standard**: Any person reviewing the evidence should be able to verify that the corrective action was completed as described.

### 5.4 Preventive Action (PA) Development and Implementation

#### 5.4.1 Preventive Action Definition

A **Preventive Action (PA)** is a specific action taken to:
- **Eliminate a potential cause** of a problem BEFORE it occurs
- **Prevent occurrence** of a nonconformance or defect
- **Reduce risk** of future quality issues

PAs are **proactive** - they address problems that have not yet occurred but could occur.

#### 5.4.2 PA Triggers

Preventive Actions are initiated when:

- **Risk Assessment identifies potential failure**: Quality Risk Management (ICH Q9) identifies a risk scenario that could impact product quality
- **Process/Equipment Change**: New equipment, material, supplier, or process that introduces potential risks
- **Design Space Expansion**: Scaling of production, change to facility, or process extension that may introduce new risks
- **Emerging Regulatory Risk**: New regulatory requirement that Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility is not yet fully prepared for
- **Competitive/Market Pressure**: Need to improve quality, reduce cost, or enhance product characteristics
- **Supplier/Material Change**: Change to suppliers or materials that may introduce inconsistency
- **Staffing Changes**: Personnel transitions that may introduce training gaps or knowledge loss

#### 5.4.3 PA Development Process

**Step 1: Identify Potential Problem**

Describe the potential problem clearly:

**Example PA Scenario**:
- **Potential Problem**: "As production volume increases to 500 kg/month, current drying room capacity may be insufficient, leading to extended drying times and potential mold contamination"
- **Risk Level**: HIGH
- **Evidence**: Current capacity 350 kg/month, forecast 500 kg/month by Q3 2024

**Step 2: Assess Probability and Impact**

Using ICH Q9 Quality Risk Management principles:

| Aspect | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Probability of Occurrence** | Medium (60% probability within 12 months if no action taken) |
| **Potential Impact if Occurs** | High (could result in batch failures, regulatory compliance issues, production delays) |
| **Overall Risk Level** | **HIGH** → Preventive action justified |

**Step 3: Preventive Action Planning**

For each potential risk, develop preventive action:

| Potential Risk | Preventive Action | Responsible | Timeline | Success Criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drying room capacity insufficient | Install additional drying racks and implement enhanced monitoring in drying room to accommodate 500 kg/month capacity | Facilities Manager | 90 days | New drying racks installed, environmental monitoring verified operational, facility validation completed per VAL-00-008, capacity testing demonstrates 500+ kg/month capability |
| Personnel unfamiliar with increased volume procedures | Conduct scenario training for Production Team on high-volume production (500 kg/month) using batch simulation | HR Manager | 60 days | Training records completed, competency assessment passed for all production personnel |
| Analytical method may not detect increased contamination risk | Conduct enhanced environmental monitoring with increased sampling frequency during high-volume pilot | QC Manager | Ongoing during scale-up | Enhanced monitoring protocol implemented, historical baseline established for comparison |

**Step 4: Preventive Action Implementation**

PAs are typically implemented **before** the risk triggers:
- Timeline is typically 30-90 days before anticipated risk
- Implementation is verified to be complete **before** operational change occurs
- Effectiveness is assessed after implementation but before full-scale risk exposure

### 5.5 Effectiveness Review

#### 5.5.1 Effectiveness Review Purpose

The **Effectiveness Review** verifies that corrective and preventive actions achieved their intended purpose:

✓ The problem does not recur (for CAs)
✓ The potential problem is prevented (for PAs)
✓ Root causes have been eliminated, not just symptoms treated
✓ Actions were implemented correctly and sustained
✓ Systemic issues are resolved
✓ Similar problems in other areas have been addressed if applicable

#### 5.5.2 Effectiveness Review Timeline

**Corrective Actions**:
- **Initial Effectiveness Check** (30 days after implementation):
  - Verify action was implemented as planned
  - Confirm no immediate signs of problem recurrence
  - Check related processes/systems are operating normally
  - Status: Implementation Verified

- **Sustained Effectiveness Review** (6 months after implementation):
  - Verify no recurrence of original problem
  - Analyze 6 months of data: test results, batch records, deviation reports
  - Confirm product quality maintained
  - Status: Effectiveness Confirmed or Effectiveness Not Confirmed

- **Ongoing Monitoring** (12 months):
  - Include in trending analysis
  - Monitor for similar issues in related processes
  - Include in management review discussion

**Preventive Actions**:
- **Initial Effectiveness Check** (30 days after implementation):
  - Verify action was implemented as planned
  - Confirm system/process functioning as designed
  - Test/validate new system with sample data or limited scope
  - Status: Implementation Verified

- **Effectiveness Validation** (when risk exposure occurs):
  - Once the anticipated risk exposure occurs, verify preventive action is effective
  - Monitor data during risk exposure period
  - Confirm problem does not occur despite risk conditions
  - Status: Effectiveness Confirmed

#### 5.5.3 Effectiveness Review Process

**Step 1: Effectiveness Review Meeting** (scheduled at 30-day and 6-month marks)

**Attendees**:
- QA Manager (facilitator)
- Responsible Person (who implemented action)
- Quality Manager (for high-risk CAPAs)
- Department Manager (if different from responsible person)
- Subject Matter Experts (if applicable)

**Meeting Agenda**:
1. Review original problem, root cause, and corrective/preventive action
2. Verify implementation: "Was action implemented as planned?"
   - Ask responsible person for evidence of implementation
   - Review implementation records
3. Assess effectiveness: "Is the problem resolved?"
   - For CA: Analyze relevant data for 30 days (or 6 months for sustained review)
   - For PA: Assess whether risk conditions could now be handled
4. Identify systemic improvements:
   - "Are similar problems occurring elsewhere?"
   - "Should this improvement be applied facility-wide?"
5. Make closure decision:
   - GREEN: Effectiveness confirmed → CAPA eligible for closure
   - YELLOW: Effectiveness uncertain → Extend monitoring or take additional actions
   - RED: Effectiveness not confirmed → Revise corrective action and reimplement

**Step 2: Data Analysis**

For Corrective Actions, analyze relevant data:

**For Product Quality Issues**:
- 6 months of test results for affected product(s)
- No OOS/OOL results
- Parameters trending stably within specification
- No customer complaints for affected product

**For Process Issues**:
- 6 months of batch records for affected process
- Critical Process Parameters (CPPs) within established range
- No yield losses or process upsets
- Environmental monitoring within acceptance criteria

**For Equipment Issues**:
- Equipment maintenance records showing no problems
- Equipment validation criteria met
- Equipment specifications still compliant
- Spare parts usage normal

**For Regulatory Issues**:
- No recurrence of same regulatory observation
- Internal audit results improved in affected area
- Regulatory agency confirmation (if applicable)

#### 5.5.4 Effectiveness Criteria

**Corrective Action is EFFECTIVE if**:
- ✓ Problem has not recurred during review period
- ✓ Root cause has been eliminated (not just symptom masked)
- ✓ Relevant product quality parameters are stable and acceptable
- ✓ Related processes/systems are functioning normally
- ✓ No new problems introduced by the corrective action
- ✓ Process improvement is sustained without increased supervision

**Preventive Action is EFFECTIVE if**:
- ✓ Risk condition occurs but expected problem does NOT occur
- ✓ System/process operates as designed under risk conditions
- ✓ Product quality maintained despite exposure to risk
- ✓ Personnel competency adequate to manage new system
- ✓ Monitoring/control systems functioning properly

### 5.6 Systemic Issues and Trending

#### 5.6.1 Systemic Issue Identification

A **Systemic Issue** is identified when:
- **Recurring Problem**: Same type of problem occurs 3+ times within 12-month period
- **Multiple Areas Affected**: Similar problem occurs in different departments/products
- **Trend Analysis**: Indicates problem pattern rather than isolated incident
- **Regulatory Indication**: Regulator notes pattern of observations
- **Management Review Identification**: Senior management identifies quality system gap

#### 5.6.2 Systemic Issue Investigation

When a systemic issue is identified:

1. **Trigger**: Quality Manager initiates new CAPA labeled "SYSTEMIC ISSUE" with broader scope
2. **Investigation Scope**: RCA investigates NOT JUST recent incident, but all 3+ occurrences
3. **Common Thread Analysis**: Identify what is common across all incidents:
   - Same equipment used?
   - Same supplier?
   - Same personnel/shift?
   - Same process step?
   - Same time period?
4. **Root Cause**: Analyze fundamental cause affecting entire group of incidents
   - Examples: Equipment design flaw, procedure inadequacy, training gap, control system deficiency
5. **Systemic Corrective Action**: Action addresses entire system, not just individual incidents
   - Examples: Equipment replacement, procedure revision facility-wide, comprehensive retraining, system redesign

#### 5.6.3 Trending Analysis and Reporting

**Quarterly Trending Review** (conducted by Quality Manager):

| Period | Metric | Value | Target | Status |
|--------|--------|-------|--------|--------|
| Q1 2024 | Total CAPA Initiations | 8 | <5 | YELLOW (above target) |
| Q1 2024 | CAPAs Closed Within Timeline | 6/8 (75%) | >90% | RED (below target) |
| Q1 2024 | Recurring Issues Identified | 2 systemic | 0 | RED (systemic issues found) |
| Q1 2024 | Average RCA Completion Time | 10 days | <7 days | YELLOW (slightly above target) |

**Trending Report Includes**:
- Total CAPAs by origin (quality, process, regulatory, preventive)
- CAPAs by department/area affected
- Primary root causes (People, Process, Plant, Materials, Methods, Environment)
- Effectiveness rate (% of CAPAs with confirmed effectiveness)
- Recurring issues and systemic problems identified
- On-time closure rate
- Trend analysis: Is CAPA volume increasing, stable, or decreasing?
- Recommendations: Should procedure/system improvements be made?

**Trending Presentation**:
- Presented in **Management Review** (QAS-01-002) at least quarterly
- Escalation trigger: If systemic issue identified, immediate presentation to Quality Manager + QP
- Used to drive continuous improvement initiatives

#### 5.7.2 CAPA Closure

A CAPA is **CLOSED** only when:

✓ Corrective action(s) fully implemented with evidence
✓ 30-day effectiveness check completed showing implementation verified
✓ 6-month sustained effectiveness review completed with confirmation of effectiveness
✓ Systemic improvements documented (if systemic issue identified)
✓ Quality Manager approval obtained
✓ Responsible persons notified of closure
✓ Updated CAPA Register entry showing closure date

**Closure Criteria NOT MET IF**:
- ✗ Action implemented but not verified
- ✗ Effectiveness uncertain (red status in review)
- ✗ New problems identified during effectiveness review
- ✗ Similar problems still occurring elsewhere (systemic issue unresolved)

In these cases, CAPA remains **OPEN** and enters:
- Revised action plan
- Extended implementation timeline
- Additional investigation
- Escalation to Quality Manager if underlying issue more complex than anticipated
