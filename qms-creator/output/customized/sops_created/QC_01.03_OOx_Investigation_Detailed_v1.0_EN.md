## 1. PURPOSE

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes a comprehensive system for **identifying, investigating, evaluating, and resolving all types of Out-of-Expected Results (OOx)** at Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility. The procedure ensures that:

- **All unexpected analytical results** are systematically investigated regardless of type (OOS, OOT, OOE, OOC)
- **Root causes are identified** and documented using structured methodologies
- **Product impact is assessed** and batch disposition determined appropriately
- **Regulatory compliance** is maintained with EU GMP, FDA, and ICH standards
- **Effectiveness of corrective actions** is verified and documented
- **Systemic issues are identified** and trending analysis performed
- **Regulatory notifications** are made when required

The OOx investigation system is critical to maintaining product quality assurance, ensuring patient safety, and demonstrating compliance with Good Manufacturing Practice requirements.

---

## 2. SCOPE

This SOP applies to **all personnel involved in analytical testing and quality control** at Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility, including:

- Quality Control Analysts (Testers)
- QC Supervisors
- Head of Quality Control
- Head of Quality Assurance
- Qualified Person (QP)
- External contracted laboratories performing testing on behalf of Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility

### 2.1 Types of Results Covered

This SOP covers investigations for **all types of unexpected results**:

| OOx Type | Scope | Triggering Event |
|----------|-------|------------------|
| **OOS** | Out-of-Specification | Test result exceeds established specification limit |
| **OOT** | Out-of-Trend | Result within spec but shows statistical drift from historical pattern |
| **OOE** | Out-of-Expectation | Unexpected result based on process knowledge and product history |
| **OOC** | Out-of-Control | Control chart/statistical limit exceeded (SPC, Z-score, Levey-Jennings) |

### 2.2 Results NOT Covered

The following are **NOT managed as OOx investigations** but through other systems:

- **Equipment malfunction errors** (immediately reported, equipment taken out of service) → EQU_06.01_v1 Equipment Management
- **Sample integrity compromised** (rejected, resampling required) → QCS_01.03_v1 Sampling SOP
- **Analyst errors caught in real-time** (not recorded as formal result) → Training/Competency
- **Transcription errors discovered before release** (corrected, not investigated as OOx)

## 3. RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

### 3.1 Quality Control Analyst (Tester)

- **Detects OOx results** during testing and immediately segregates sample/data
- **Completes Phase IA checklist** (Annex A01, Section 2)
- **Notifies QC Supervisor immediately** for all OOx detections
- **Preserves evidence**: Original data, sample, standards, equipment records
- **Participates in Phase IB** (7-step assessment) with supervisor
- **Completes investigation record** with detailed observations and data
- **Implements approved corrective actions** as assigned
- **Documents all activities** in OOx investigation record

**Authority Limits:**
- ❌ Cannot determine batch release status
- ❌ Cannot close investigation
- ❌ Cannot make regulatory notifications
- ✅ Can recommend containment actions
- ✅ Can suggest corrective actions

### 3.2 QC Supervisor

- **Reviews Phase IA checklist** completed by analyst
- **Leads Phase IB 7-step assessment** with analyst and subject matter experts
- **Assigns investigation categorization** (Phase I vs. Phase II required)
- **Determines investigation timeline** (HIGH: 20 days, MEDIUM: 30 days, LOW: 15 days)
- **Signs Phase I assessment** and approves investigation record
- **Escalates HIGH-risk findings** to Head of QC immediately
- **Monitors action deadlines** and follow-up requirements

**Authority Limits:**
- ❌ Cannot release batch in presence of OOx
- ❌ Can only approve LOW-risk closure (per QA Manager authority)
- ✅ Can temporarily segregate/hold product
- ✅ Can implement immediate containment

### 3.3 Head of Quality Control

- **Reviews complete Phase I investigation** and assessment
- **Determines if Phase II investigation required**
- **Approves containment and corrective action plan**
- **Ensures investigation timeline adherence**
- **Reviews effectiveness evidence** at 30-day and 6-month intervals
- **Signs investigation record** as "Reviewed by"

**Authority Limits:**
- ❌ Cannot approve batch release (QP decision only)
- ❌ Cannot determine regulatory notification requirement
- ✅ Can approve Phase II investigation scope
- ✅ Can recommend preventive actions

### 3.4 Head of Quality Assurance

- **Provides professional opinion** on root cause and appropriateness of corrective actions
- **Reviews investigation rationale** and risk assessment
- **Determines regulatory notification requirement**
- **Assesses systemic implications** of OOx findings
- **Signs investigation record** as "Reviewed by" (opinion)
- **Escalates to QP** if regulatory notification likely

**Authority Limits:**
- ❌ Cannot overrule QP batch release decision
- ✅ Can recommend investigation expansion
- ✅ Can suggest systemic improvements

### 3.5 Qualified Person (QP)

- **Reviews complete investigation** including all phases and evidence
- **Makes final batch disposition decision**:
  - ☐ **RELEASE** - with or without conditions
  - ☐ **RETAIN** - pending additional investigation
  - ☐ **REJECT/DESTROY** - unfit for intended use
- **Approves investigation record** with final signature
- **Authorizes regulatory notifications** if required
- **Signs off on corrective actions** effectiveness verification
- **Has final authority** on all OOx matters affecting batch release

**Authority Limits:**
- ✅ Absolute authority over batch disposition
- ✅ Can override any recommendation
- ✅ Can require additional investigation
- ❌ Cannot release without documented investigation (GMP requirement)

### 3.6 External Contracted Laboratories

- **Perform testing per Quality Testing Agreement (QTA)**
- **Immediately notify Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility** if OOx detected (within 24 hours)
- **Complete Annex A02 investigation record** per QTA requirements
- **Provide detailed investigation report** with root cause analysis
- **Implement corrective actions** per Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility approval
- **Verify effectiveness** with follow-up testing

## 4. DEFINITIONS AND OOx TYPES

### 4.1 Out-of-Specification (OOS)

**Definition:** A test result that **exceeds the established specification limit** defined in the product monograph or Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility specification.

**Characteristics:**
- Result is definitively outside accepted range
- Specification is clear, documented, and regulatory-compliant
- Detection is objective and measurable
- Batch potentially unsuitable for intended use unless investigation proves otherwise

**Examples (Cannabis Testing):**
- ❌ **THC Content**: Specification NMT 22% (from Ph.Eur. 3028), measured 23.5%
- ❌ **Microbial Limit**: Specification ≤1×10³ CFU/mL Total Aerobic, measured 2.5×10³ CFU/mL
- ❌ **Aflatoxin B1**: Specification NMT 2 µg/kg, measured 3.2 µg/kg
- ❌ **Moisture Content**: Specification 8-12%, measured 7.2%

**Scope/Intended Use:**
- OOS is the primary trigger for formal investigation
- Used when quality attribute verification fails
- Indicates potential batch non-conformance
- Usually requires Phase I investigation minimum

**Correct Execution:**
1. Confirm specification is correct and up-to-date
2. Verify test method is valid and equipment calibrated
3. Re-analyze if within analytical uncertainty (±10% per Ph.Eur. 3028)
4. Document all verification steps
5. Initiate formal Phase IA checklist

**Records:**
- Initial OOx Notification (Annex A04, Part A)
- Phase IA Investigation Checklist (Annex A01, Section 2)
- OOx Register entry (Annex A03)

---

### 4.2 Out-of-Trend (OOT)

**Definition:** A test result that is **within specification limits but shows unexpected deviation from established statistical trend** based on historical batch data.

**Characteristics:**
- Result may be within specification
- Trend shows statistically significant change from historical baseline
- Indicates potential process drift or control issue
- May precede OOS if trend continues
- Early warning system for quality drift

**Examples (Cannabis Testing):**
- ⚠️ **CBD Content Drift**: Historical average 8.5% ±0.3% per batch; last 6 batches trending downward (8.5% → 8.3% → 8.1% → 7.9% → 7.7% → 7.5%)
- ⚠️ **Microbial Count Increase**: Historical <100 CFU/mL; last 4 batches trending up (150 → 280 → 420 → 650 CFU/mL, still <1000 limit)
- ⚠️ **Moisture Content Rising**: Historical 10.2% ±0.2%; batch 1-5 trending 10.2% → 10.4% → 10.6% → 10.8% → 11.0%
- ⚠️ **Potency Inconsistency**: Strain historically 18-20% THC; last 3 batches 18.5% → 17.2% → 15.8%

**Scope/Intended Use:**
- Detects emerging quality issues before they become OOS
- Proactive quality management tool
- Triggers investigation to identify and correct root cause
- Prevents future OOS results through early intervention
- Demonstrates product quality consistency over time

**Correct Execution:**
1. Establish baseline and control limits from historical data (minimum 20 batches recommended)
2. Calculate statistical limits (mean ±2-3 standard deviations recommended)
3. Monitor each batch result against trend
4. When threshold exceeded, investigate immediately
5. Determine if trend indicates: Process drift, equipment wear, material change, technique variation, or environmental change
6. Implement corrective action to restore trend

**Records:**
- Trend analysis documentation with statistics
- Phase IA Investigation Checklist (Annex A01, Section 2)
- OOx Register entry (Annex A03)
- OOT-specific root cause analysis

---

### 4.3 Out-of-Expectation (OOE)

**Definition:** A test result that **is within specification but contradicts expected result** based on process knowledge, product history, or quality attributes.

**Characteristics:**
- Result meets specification requirement
- Expectation is based on process understanding and prior batches
- Inconsistency suggests unexpected variation or process change
- May indicate uncontrolled variable affecting product
- Requires investigation to determine if process change occurred

**Examples (Cannabis Testing):**
- ⚠️ **Unexpected High Potency**: High-CBD strain typically yields 6-8% CBD; batch suddenly produces 10.2% CBD (spec allows NMT 12%)
- ⚠️ **Unexpected Low Cannabinoid Profile**: Mother plant clones typically produce consistent THCA/CBDA ratio; one batch shows significantly different ratio
- ⚠️ **Unexpected Terpene Profile**: Strain known for specific terpene profile (myrcene dominant); GC analysis shows unexpected terpene distribution
- ⚠️ **Unexpected Color/Appearance**: Visually assessed dried flower color differs from historical batches (within specification but atypical)
- ⚠️ **Unexpected Microbial Profile**: Environmental sampling shows different organism than historical pattern (still within limits)

**Scope/Intended Use:**
- Identifies unexpected process variations
- Detects uncontrolled variables affecting product
- Indicates potential process drift or changes
- Helps optimize process understanding and control
- Prevents similar issues in future batches

**Correct Execution:**
1. Define what "expected" means (historical data, specification, process knowledge)
2. Document the expectation clearly
3. Verify result accuracy (method validity, equipment, conditions)
4. Investigate cause of deviation from expectation:
   - Did cultivation conditions change?
   - Was material from different harvest lot used?
   - Did drying/processing parameters vary?
   - Did environmental conditions change?
   - Did equipment performance change?
5. Assess if deviation acceptable or requires intervention
6. Implement preventive actions if similar issue could recur

**Records:**
- Expectation documentation (why result unexpected)
- Phase IA Investigation Checklist (Annex A01, Section 2)
- OOx Register entry (Annex A03)
- Root cause analysis focused on process changes

---

### 4.4 Out-of-Control (OOC)

**Definition:** A test result that **exceeds statistical control limits** established on control charts or statistical process control (SPC) limits.

**Characteristics:**
- Result may be within specification
- Exceeds control limit (typically mean ±3 standard deviations, or Z-score >3)
- Indicates process has special cause variation
- Not expected under normal process operation
- Requires investigation to identify and eliminate special cause

**Examples (Cannabis Testing):**
- ⚠️ **Control Chart Excursion**: Reference standard chromatogram shows peak at expected retention time; analytical control shows +3σ deviation (result valid but process drifted)
- ⚠️ **Z-Score Elevation**: Proficiency testing sample result has Z-score of -3.2 (acceptable accuracy but statistical anomaly)
- ⚠️ **Levey-Jennings Rule Violation**: Quality control result on control chart shows 2 consecutive results >2σ from mean (process approaching out-of-control)
- ⚠️ **Duplicate Analysis Variance**: Two consecutive analyses of same sample differ by more than expected precision (possible equipment drift)

**Scope/Intended Use:**
- Statistical tool for detecting process instability
- Identifies subtle shifts in analytical performance
- Enables early detection before product quality affected
- Used with continuous monitoring (control charts, trending)
- Demonstrates process capability (Cpk) maintenance

**Correct Execution:**
1. Establish control limits from baseline data (minimum 25 measurements recommended)
2. Implement control charting (Levey-Jennings or XmR chart typical)
3. Use decision rules for OOC detection:
   - Single result >3σ from mean
   - 2 consecutive results >2σ from mean (same side)
   - 4 consecutive results >1σ from mean (same side)
   - 10 consecutive results >0σ from mean (trend)
4. When rule violated, stop testing and investigate
5. Identify special cause (equipment issue, reagent change, environmental change, technique change)
6. Implement corrective action
7. Verify control restored before resuming routine testing

**Records:**
- Control chart with OOC point highlighted
- Phase IA Investigation Checklist (Annex A01, Section 2)
- OOx Register entry (Annex A03)
- Control limit recalculation if appropriate

## 5. INVESTIGATION PROCEDURE

### 5.1 Overview

All OOx investigations follow a **three-phase structured approach**:

| Phase | Duration | Responsibility | Scope |
|-------|----------|-----------------|-------|
| **Phase IA** | Immediate (0-24 hrs) | QC Analyst + Supervisor | Preliminary checklist investigation |
| **Phase IB** | 3-7 days | QC Supervisor + Head of QC | 7-step supervisor assessment |
| **Phase II** | Extended (varies) | Investigation Team | Full root cause analysis if required |

**Investigation Timeline Based on Risk:**

| Risk Level | Timeline | Approval | Justification |
|-----------|----------|----------|---------------|
| **HIGH** | 20 working days | QP Required | Patient safety concern, regulatory implication, or released product |
| **MEDIUM** | 30 working days | Head of QC + QA review | Potential batch impact, corrective action needed |
| **LOW** | 15 working days | QC Supervisor + QA review | No batch impact likely, procedural issue |

---

### 5.2 Phase IA: Immediate Analyst Response (Within 24 Hours)

**Objective:** Preliminary assessment and data preservation

**Responsibilities:**
- QC Analyst who detected OOx
- QC Supervisor
- Potentially: Department Manager, Production Manager (if cultivation/processing related)

**Actions:**

**Step 1: Immediate Segregation and Notification (Within 2 Hours)**
- ☐ **Segregate sample**: Mark as "HOLD - OOx INVESTIGATION" or physical separate if possible
- ☐ **Secure data**: Original test data, chromatograms, instrument records
- ☐ **Preserve evidence**: Standards used, reagents (lot numbers, expiry), equipment records
- ☐ **Notify QC Supervisor** immediately (do not wait for end of day or shift)
- ☐ **Notify Head of QC** for HIGH-risk findings
- ☐ **Initial batch notification**: Inform Production/Warehouse if batch in circulation

**Step 2: Complete Phase IA Checklist (Within 24 Hours)**

Use **Annex A01, Section 2: Phase IA - Analyst Immediate Review Checklist**

**Laboratory Data Verification (100+ questions covering):**
- ☐ Sample receipt: Date, time, identity, integrity upon receipt
- ☐ Sample preparation: All steps documented, deviations noted
- ☐ Analytical method: Correct procedure used, method validation current
- ☐ Equipment: ID, calibration status, maintenance records
- ☐ Reagents: ID, lot number, expiry, storage conditions
- ☐ Standards: Reference standard identity, purity, expiry, storage
- ☐ Environmental conditions: Temperature, humidity during analysis
- ☐ Analyst: Name, qualifications, recent training
- ☐ Data documentation: Original vs. transcribed, calculations verified
- ☐ Quality control: Reference standards, quality control samples, blanks
- ☐ Equipment performance: Control charts, maintenance performed recently
- ☐ Method validation: Results within acceptance criteria
- ☐ Calculations: Mathematical verification, unit conversions
- ☐ Instrument performance: Response factors, calibration curves

**Product/Process Data Verification (50+ questions covering):**
- ☐ Batch identity: Batch number, product code, batch size
- ☐ Batch record review: All processing steps documented
- ☐ Raw material: Supplier, lot number, acceptance testing
- ☐ Environmental monitoring: Temperature, humidity, GMP classification
- ☐ Personnel: Who handled material, training records
- ☐ Equipment used in manufacturing: Maintenance status, calibration
- ☐ Time relationships: Timing of harvest, drying, storage, testing
- ☐ Previous batches: Similar results or anomalies?
- ☐ Cultivation parameters: Lighting, nutrients, irrigation, temperature
- ☐ Harvesting timing: Maturity assessment, trichome condition
- ☐ Drying parameters: Temperature, humidity, duration (cannabis-specific)
- ☐ Storage conditions: Temperature, humidity, packaging material

**Initial Findings:**
- ☐ Probable cause(s) identified
- ☐ Preliminary assessment (likely process drift, equipment issue, sample issue, etc.)
- ☐ Questions for further investigation
- ☐ Evidence secured and documented

**Step 3: Risk Classification**

Categorize OOx as HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW based on:

**HIGH Risk Criteria (if ANY present):**
- ☑ Batch already released to customers/market
- ☑ Result affects Critical Quality Attribute (CQA)
- ☑ Patient safety potential impact (contaminant detected, potency variance >10% specification)
- ☑ Multiple batches affected
- ☑ OOS result (by definition High)
- ☑ Regulatory limit exceeded or implication
- ☑ Third-time occurrence of same issue in 12 months

**MEDIUM Risk Criteria (if ANY present):**
- ☑ Batch in final stages of release (not yet shipped)
- ☑ OOT result with clear trend
- ☑ OOE suggesting process change
- ☑ OOC indicating control loss
- ☑ Second occurrence of same issue in 12 months
- ☑ Corrective action clearly needed

**LOW Risk Criteria (if ALL present):**
- ✓ Batch not released
- ✓ Batch within specification range
- ✓ Singular occurrence (first time)
- ✓ Likely procedural/transcription issue
- ✓ Readily apparent corrective action
- ✓ No process concern

---

### 5.3 Phase IB: 7-Step Supervisor Assessment (3-7 Days)

**Objective:** Structured supervisor evaluation and preliminary root cause identification

**Responsibility:** QC Supervisor (with analyst input and SME consultation)

**Process:**

**Step 1: Re-Analysis (If Applicable)**
- ☐ Re-test original sample using same method
- ☐ Re-test backup sample if available
- ☐ If results confirm: OOx verified, proceed with investigation
- ☐ If results differ significantly: Analytical variance issue identified
- ☐ Document all re-analysis results and conditions

**Step 2: Method Verification**
- ☐ Verify method is appropriate for sample type
- ☐ Confirm method parameters match product specification
- ☐ Review method validation documentation
- ☐ Assess analytical uncertainty (e.g., Ph.Eur. 3028 ±10% tolerance)
- ☐ Determine if result truly OOx or within analytical uncertainty
- ☐ If within uncertainty, document conclusion and close investigation

**Step 3: Equipment Verification**
- ☐ Calibration status current? (verify calibration records)
- ☐ Equipment maintenance current? (verify maintenance log)
- ☐ Equipment performance acceptable? (verify control chart/trending)
- ☐ Equipment used for this analysis is qualified? (IQ/OQ/PQ records)
- ☐ If equipment issue found: repair/recalibration, retest, reassess
- ☐ Document all equipment findings

**Step 4: Reagent/Standard Verification**
- ☐ Reference standard: correct identity, purity, within expiry?
- ☐ Reagents: correct identity, proper storage, within expiry?
- ☐ Quality control samples: correct preparation, proper storage?
- ☐ Blanks: prepared correctly, analyzed correctly?
- ☐ If reagent issue found: replace, retest, reassess
- ☐ Document all reagent findings

**Step 5: Analyst Technique Assessment**
- ☐ Analyst competency verified? (training records, annual competency assessment)
- ☐ Recent technique changes or variations?
- ☐ Analyst performance on quality control samples acceptable?
- ☐ Potential technique issue identified from checklist?
- ☐ If technique issue: retrain, retest, reassess
- ☐ Document analyst findings and any training recommendations

**Step 6: Data Verification and Calculations**
- ☐ Manual calculations checked independently
- ☐ Unit conversions verified
- ☐ Data transcription verified (original vs. reported)
- ☐ Significant figures appropriate?
- ☐ If calculation error found: correct, reassess if still OOx
- ☐ Document all calculation findings

**Step 7: Preliminary Root Cause Assessment**
- ☐ Summarize findings from Steps 1-6
- ☐ Identify preliminary root cause(s):
  - ☐ Analytical issue (method, equipment, technique, reagent)
  - ☐ Sampling issue (wrong sample, compromised integrity)
  - ☐ Calculation/transcription issue
  - ☐ Process/product issue (suspected, requires Phase II investigation)
  - ☐ Environmental issue
  - ☐ Unknown (requires Phase II investigation)
- ☐ Recommend Phase II investigation if root cause NOT identified
- ☐ Recommend corrective action if root cause identified and addressable
- ☐ Document all findings and recommendations

### 5.4 Phase II: Extended Investigation (If Required)

**Objective:** Comprehensive root cause analysis for complex issues

**When Required:**
- Root cause NOT identified in Phase IB
- Multiple factors contributing
- Systemic issue suspected
- Historical correlation suggesting pattern
- QP requires extended investigation for batch release decision

**Process:**

**Investigation Team Composition:**
- QC Supervisor (Lead)
- Head of QC
- Head of QA
- Subject Matter Experts (e.g., Production Manager, Cultivation Manager, Equipment Specialist)
- External expert if specialized knowledge needed

**Investigation Scope (Select Appropriate):**

**1. Process Investigation**
- ☐ Cultivation parameters review: Lighting, nutrients, irrigation, temperature, humidity, light spectrum
- ☐ Harvesting timing and assessment: Maturity, trichome condition, timing
- ☐ Drying process: Temperature profile, humidity control, duration, equipment used
- ☐ Processing: Trimming, cleaning, grinding, packaging parameters
- ☐ Storage: Temperature, humidity, packaging integrity, time in storage
- ☐ Batch record detailed review: All steps, all parameters, all deviations

**2. Equipment Investigation (if process-related)**
- ☐ All equipment used in manufacturing: Maintenance records, calibration records, performance trending
- ☐ Environmental monitoring: Room temperature/humidity, microbial monitoring results
- ☐ Utility systems: HVAC performance, water systems, compressed air
- ☐ Equipment qualification: URS, IQ, OQ, PQ records current
- ☐ Cleaning validation: Residue testing, validation reports

**3. Material Investigation (if input-related)**
- ☐ Raw material supplier: Historical performance, recent changes
- ☐ Raw material lot: Acceptance testing results, storage conditions
- ☐ Packaging materials: Supplier performance, interaction studies
- ☐ Reagents/standards: Supplier changes, lot variations

**4. Statistical/Trending Investigation**
- ☐ Historical batch data: Plotting all previous results for this test
- ☐ Trend analysis: Linear regression, moving averages, control charting
- ☐ Comparison: Results by equipment, by analyst, by time, by season
- ☐ Capability analysis: Process Cpk calculation, stability assessment

**5. Personnel/Training Investigation**
- ☐ Analyst training records: Initial training, competency assessment, refresher training
- ☐ Procedure compliance: Walk-through observation, procedure familiarity assessment
- ☐ Incident investigation: Any health issues, medication, or unusual circumstances?
- ☐ Training adequacy: Procedure clarity, training frequency, assessment methods

**6. External Contractor Investigation (if outsourced testing)**
- Use **Annex A02: External Contractor OOx Investigation Record**
- ☐ Laboratory qualifications: ISO 17025 scope, accreditation status
- ☐ Quality Testing Agreement: Requirements, responsibilities, reporting
- ☐ Testing documentation: QTA compliance, method validation, quality controls
- ☐ Contractor's investigation: Root cause analysis, corrective action recommendations
- ☐ Contractor approval: Re-qualification if needed

**Deliverables:**
- Comprehensive root cause analysis report
- Statistical trending analysis if applicable
- Corrective action plan with timeline
- Preventive action recommendations
- Risk assessment: Likelihood of recurrence, severity if recurs

## 6. OOx TYPE-SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS

### 6.1 OOS-Specific Procedure

**Mandatory Requirements:**

**Regulatory Reporting:**
- ☐ All OOS results are **presumed HIGH RISK** until proven otherwise
- ☐ Automatic escalation to QP and regulatory considerations
- ☐ Timeline: Investigation completion within 20 working days

**Investigation Focus:**
- ☐ Determination: Is batch unfit for intended use due to this parameter?
- ☐ Patient impact: Could customer/patient be harmed by this OOS attribute?
- ☐ Regulatory impact: Does OOS violate regulatory requirement?
- ☐ Historical context: Previous OOS results for this attribute?

**Batch Disposition Options:**
1. **RELEASE** - Only if investigation proves OOx was analytical/procedural error, not actual product issue
2. **RELEASE WITH CONDITIONS** - Testing alternative method, supplier review, or enhanced monitoring
3. **RETAIN** - Pending additional investigation or retesting
4. **REJECT** - Batch unfit, returned to supplier, or destroyed per SOP

**Regulatory Notification Assessment Required** (Annex A04, Part D)

---

### 6.2 OOT-Specific Procedure

**Mandatory Requirements:**

**Trend Establishment:**
- ☐ Establish baseline: Minimum 20 consecutive batches for statistical validity
- ☐ Control limits: Mean ±2-3 standard deviations (recommended)
- ☐ Trend direction: Clearly identify direction and rate of drift
- ☐ Statistical significance: Apply statistical test (e.g., run test, Mann-Whitney)

**Investigation Focus:**
- ☐ Process drift: Is this normal variation or unusual trend?
- ☐ Environmental factors: Seasonal effects, ambient conditions?
- ☐ Material variation: Supplier changes, harvest timing, storage?
- ☐ Equipment aging: Gradual performance decline over time?
- ☐ Technique evolution: Analyst experience, procedure interpretation?

**Corrective Action Emphasis:**
- Prevent progression to OOS
- Identify and correct underlying cause
- Restore trend to baseline
- Implement preventive control

**Timeline:**
- LOW-MEDIUM Risk (usually): 30 working days acceptable
- Immediate investigation if trending toward specification limit

---

### 6.3 OOE-Specific Procedure

**Mandatory Requirements:**

**Expectation Definition:**
- ☐ Clearly state what was "expected" (product history, specification, process knowledge)
- ☐ Provide evidence: Historical data, precedent batches, design parameters
- ☐ Justify expectation: Why this result unexpected?

**Investigation Focus:**
- ☐ Uncontrolled variables: What changed from normal operation?
- ☐ Cultivation parameters: Lighting cycle, nutrients, climate?
- ☐ Process changes: Procedure variation, equipment change, handling differences?
- ☐ Timing: Different maturity, extended storage, different season?
- ☐ Supply chain: Different cultivar source, cuttings vs. seed?

**Corrective Action Emphasis:**
- Restore product consistency and predictability
- Control identified variables
- Prevent similar issues in future batches
- May be LOW RISK if result acceptable and corrected

---

### 6.4 OOC-Specific Procedure

**Mandatory Requirements:**

**Control Limits Validation:**
- ☐ Control limits current and statistically valid?
- ☐ Based on adequate baseline data (minimum 25 measurements)?
- ☐ Process stable during baseline establishment?
- ☐ Appropriate decision rules for control assessment?

**Investigation Focus:**
- ☐ Special cause identification: What unusual event caused this excursion?
- ☐ Equipment performance: Drift, aging, maintenance issue?
- ☐ Reagent/standard changes: New lot, different storage condition?
- ☐ Environmental changes: Temperature, humidity deviation?
- ☐ Analytical technique: Variation in technique, timing, conditions?

**Immediate Actions:**
- ☐ Stop testing immediately (do not process further samples)
- ☐ Investigate before resuming routine testing
- ☐ Take corrective action (equipment service, reagent replacement, etc.)
- ☐ Verify control restored (analyze control sample, confirm result within limits)
- ☐ Resume testing only when control confirmed

## 9. CANNABIS-SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS

### 9.1 Cannabinoid Analysis Per Ph.Eur. Monograph 3028

**Key Parameters:**

| Parameter | Specification | Tolerance | Notes |
|-----------|---|---|---|
| **THC (Delta-9-Tetrahydrocannabinol)** | NMT 1% | ±10% | EU cannabis definition threshold |
| **THCA (THC Acid)** | Specified per product | ±10% | Primary active in fresh flower |
| **CBD (Cannabidiol)** | NMT limit product-specific | ±10% | Therapeutic component |
| **CBDA (CBD Acid)** | Specified per product | ±10% | Primary in fresh flower |
| **Total THC** | Per specification | ±10% | Includes THCA converted to THC |
| **Total CBD** | Per specification | ±10% | Includes CBDA converted to CBD |

**THCA-to-THC Conversion Factor:**
- **Conversion factor: 0.877** (multiply THCA by 0.877 to get equivalent THC content)
- Used in calculation: Total THC = THC + (THCA × 0.877)
- Critical for batch potency classification and compliance

**OOS Example:**
- Product specification: "Total THC NMT 1%" (EU legal medical cannabis definition)
- Test result: THCA 0.95%, THC 0.15%
- Calculation: Total THC = 0.15 + (0.95 × 0.877) = 0.15 + 0.83 = 0.98%
- Assessment: ✓ Within specification (0.98% < 1.0%)
- Note: ±10% tolerance means range 0.90-1.10% acceptable for specification

---

### 9.2 Trichome Maturity and Harvest Assessment

**Cannabis Plant Health Considerations:**

**Trichome Maturity (NOT formal IPC - see separate SOP)**
- Affects cannabinoid profile (THCA vs. THC)
- Affects terpene profile (aromatic compounds)
- Observed trichome color: Clear → Cloudy → Amber progression
- Affects product potency and effects profile
- Historical variation: Different strains, environmental conditions

**If OOE Related to Unexpected Cannabinoid Profile:**
- ☐ Review harvest maturity assessment records
- ☐ Compare trichome observations to previous batches
- ☐ Environmental conditions during flowering (light, temperature, humidity)
- ☐ Time from harvest to drying/analysis
- ☐ Storage conditions (light, temperature, humidity) - affects THCA → THC conversion

**OOT Example:**
- Historical: Strain consistently produces 18-20% total THC
- Batch 1-5 trending: 20%, 19.8%, 19.5%, 19.2%, 18.8%
- Analysis: Possible earlier harvest (less THCA development) or storage-induced THCA conversion
- Corrective action: Verify harvest maturity standards, confirm storage conditions

---

### 9.3 Environmental Monitoring and Contamination Risk

**Critical Parameters for Cannabis Production:**

| Parameter | Target Range | Impact if Out of Spec | Cannabis-Specific Concern |
|-----------|---|---|---|
| **Temperature** | 18-26°C | Cannabinoid/terpene profile changes | Affects potency development |
| **Humidity** | 45-55% | Mold risk, THCA conversion rate | Botrytis risk in flowering |
| **Air Changes** | 6-8 per hour | Stagnant air, mold, pest issues | Critical in dense canopy |
| **Light Cycle** | Strain-specific | Flowering duration, potency | Photoperiod-dependent |

**Microbial Contamination:**
- **Aspergillus** (mycotoxins - aflatoxins, ochratoxins)
- **Botrytis** (gray mold - Ph.Eur. requires specific testing)
- **Bacterial counts** (aerobic and anaerobic)
- **Yeast and mold** (general indicators)

**If Microbial OOS Detected:**
- ☐ Environmental monitoring records during cultivation
- ☐ Drying conditions review (temperature/humidity profile)
- ☐ Processing/trimming hygiene assessment
- ☐ Packaging material cleanliness
- ☐ Storage facility GMP classification review
- ☐ Previous batches microbial test results (OOT assessment)

---

### 9.4 Trichome Distribution and Sample Homogeneity

**Sampling Challenge for Cannabis Flower:**

**Variable Cannabinoid Distribution:**
- Trichomes concentrate on outer surfaces of flower
- Interior vs. exterior cannabinoid content varies
- Flower density affects cannabinoid distribution
- Grinding/processing homogenizes sample

**If Unexpected Potency Variation:**
- ☐ Sample preparation review: Grinding uniformity, particle size consistency
- ☐ Subsampling procedure: Random selection, representative?
- ☐ Sample size: Adequate for heterogeneous matrix?
- ☐ Replicate analysis: If high variance between replicates, sample homogeneity suspect
- ☐ Batch mixing: Was batch thoroughly mixed before sampling?

**OOE Example:**
- Expected strain potency: 18% THC
- First subsample: 19.2%
- Second subsample: 16.8%
- Average: 18.0% (specification met)
- Analysis: High variation suggests inadequate sample homogeneity
- Corrective action: Improve batch mixing and sampling procedure

## 10. REGULATORY NOTIFICATION PROCEDURES

### 10.1 Regulatory Trigger Assessment

**Evaluate Each HIGH-Risk OOx Against 10 Regulatory Triggers:**

**Trigger 1: Potency Threshold**
- ☐ Does OOx involve THC content exceeding EU medical cannabis legal limit (>1%)?
- ☐ Impact: Reclassification as controlled substance, regulatory implications

**Trigger 2: Contaminant Detection**
- ☐ Does OOx involve detection of prohibited substance (pesticide, heavy metal, solvent)?
- ☐ Impact: Public health risk, regulatory violation, potential customer notification

**Trigger 3: Microbial Contamination**
- ☐ Does OOx involve microbial count exceeding pharmacopeial limits?
- ☐ Does it involve Aspergillus (mycotoxin risk) or Botrytis detection?
- ☐ Impact: Patient safety risk (immunocompromised, respiratory patients)

**Trigger 4: Batch Release Status**
- ☐ Has batch already been released to customers/market?
- ☐ Impact: Immediate notification required if risk identified

**Trigger 5: Multiple Batches Affected**
- ☐ Do multiple batches show same OOx?
- ☐ Impact: Systemic issue, broader market impact

**Trigger 6: Critical Quality Attribute**
- ☐ Does OOx affect product identity or critical characteristic?
- ☐ Impact: Product safety or efficacy compromised

**Trigger 7: Previous Similar Issue**
- ☐ Does OOx register show similar issue previously?
- ☐ Impact: Systemic problem not adequately resolved

**Trigger 8: Regulatory Authority Inquiry**
- ☐ Is regulator currently investigating similar issue?
- ☐ Impact: Notification requirement, transparency obligation

**Trigger 9: Patient Safety Potential**
- ☐ Could this OOx cause patient harm if product used as-is?
- ☐ Impact: Immediate escalation, potential recall consideration

**Trigger 10: Specification Alignment with Regulation**
- ☐ Is specification compliant with EU GMP and EudraLex requirements?
- ☐ If OOx, does it indicate regulatory non-compliance by Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility?

---

### 10.2 Regulatory Notification Decision Tree

**If ANY Trigger Present AND Batch Released → LIKELY REGULATORY NOTIFICATION REQUIRED**

**Notification Pathway:**
1. **Head of QA** → Initiates Annex A04, Part D assessment
2. **QP** → Determines if notification legally required
3. **Regulatory Affairs** → Submits notification to appropriate authority:
   - **EU EMA** - If Nordic/German/DK facility (medicinal product notifications)
   - **National Health Ministry** - Country-specific requirements
   - **Police/Drug Enforcement** - If legal threshold exceeded
   - **Customers** - If already distributed, customer notification per QTA

**Notification Timeline:**
- **Immediate** (within 24 hours): If patient safety risk or potential serious adverse event
- **5 working days**: For serious quality issues affecting released product
- **15 working days**: For standard regulatory notifications
