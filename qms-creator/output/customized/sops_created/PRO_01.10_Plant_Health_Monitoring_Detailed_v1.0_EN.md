## 1. PURPOSE

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes a systematic framework for **monitoring plant health throughout the complete cannabis cultivation cycle** at Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility. The monitoring system ensures that:

- **Critical cultivation parameters** are maintained within specification
- **Plant health status** is assessed and documented at each growth phase
- **Deviations** from optimal conditions are detected early and corrected
- **Production efficiency** is optimized through proactive health management
- **Product quality** is maintained through appropriate growing conditions
- **Regulatory compliance** with GACP (Good Agricultural Practice) is demonstrated
- **Traceability and documentation** support batch record completion and quality assurance

---

## 2. SCOPE

This SOP applies to:

- **All cannabis plants** grown at Purely Plant Medical Cannabis Production Facility, including:
  - Commercial production plants (cloning → vegetative → flowering → harvest)
  - Mother plants (propagation and maintenance)
  - Selection/R&D plants (strain development and phenotype evaluation)

- **All cultivation phases**:
  - Cloning & Rooting (7-14 days)
  - Vegetative Phase (3-8 weeks)
  - Flowering Phase (8-12 weeks depending on strain)
  - Harvest Phase (assessment and harvesting)

- **All personnel** involved in cultivation and plant health monitoring

## 3. RESPONSIBILITIES

### 3.1 Cultivation Manager

- Oversees all plant health monitoring activities
- Establishes critical parameters and tolerances per strain
- Approves corrective actions for deviations
- Ensures training and competency of cultivation staff
- Conducts management review of plant health data
- Approves batch records based on monitoring documentation

### 3.2 Cultivation Team Lead

- Daily monitoring and recording of plant health observations
- Implementation of monitoring procedures per this SOP
- Immediate notification of critical deviations
- Documentation of all monitoring activities
- Preliminary assessment of plant health status

### 3.3 Cultivation Technicians

- Participate in daily monitoring activities
- Record observations and measurements
- Report deviations immediately to Team Lead
- Implement corrective actions as directed
- Maintain monitoring equipment (meters, gauges, thermometers)

### 3.4 Facility Manager (Environment)

- Maintains environmental monitoring systems
- Ensures HVAC systems operating within specification
- Records temperature, humidity, CO₂, light parameters
- Notifies Cultivation Manager of environmental excursions
- Conducts preventive maintenance on environmental systems

### 3.5 Quality Assurance Manager

- Conducts periodic audits of monitoring procedures
- Reviews plant health monitoring records
- Validates that documentation supports batch completion
- Identifies systemic issues requiring corrective action
- Approves CAPA when plant health contributes to batch issues

## 4. DEFINITIONS

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **GACP** | Good Agricultural and Collection Practices (EU guideline for medicinal plant cultivation) |
| **Plant Health Status** | Overall condition of plant assessed through visual, environmental, and parameter monitoring |
| **Critical Parameter** | Environmental or plant condition factor that significantly affects product quality |
| **Deviation** | Observation or measurement outside defined acceptable range for phase/condition |
| **Phenotype** | Observable characteristics of plant resulting from genetics and environment interaction |
| **Cannabinoid Profile** | Ratio of THC, THCA, CBD, CBDA, and other cannabinoids in plant tissue |
| **Trichome Maturity** | Development stage of cannabis resin glands (Clear → Cloudy → Amber) |
| **VPD** | Vapor Pressure Deficit (environmental parameter affecting plant transpiration) |

| **IPM** | Integrated Pest Management (non-chemical pest control strategies) |

## 5. GENERAL MONITORING PROCEDURE

### 5.1 Monitoring Frequency by Phase

| Phase | Monitoring Frequency | Critical Parameters | Documentation |
|-------|---------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Cloning & Rooting** | Daily (2x daily for rooting) | Humidity, Temperature, Light, Root emergence | Daily Log |
| **Vegetative** | Daily (minimum) | Light, Temperature, Humidity, Nutrients, Plant height, Leaf condition | Daily Log + Weekly Summary |
| **Flowering** | Daily (critical last 3 weeks) | Light cycle, Temperature, Humidity, Nutrient uptake, Flower development, Pest/disease | Daily Log + Weekly Summary |
| **Harvest** | At ripeness assessment | Trichome maturity, Overall plant appearance, Mold/pest evidence | Pre-harvest assessment |

### 5.2 Environmental Monitoring

**Daily Environmental Monitoring Required:**

| Parameter | Target Range | Measurement Frequency | Action if Out of Range |
|-----------|--------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| **Temperature** | 18-26°C day / 16-20°C night | Continuous or 3x daily | Adjust HVAC, notify Facility Manager |
| **Humidity** | 40-60% (veg), 45-55% (flower) | Continuous or 3x daily | Adjust HVAC/dehumidifier, notify Facility Manager |
| **Light Intensity** | 400-1200 µmol/m²/s | 1x daily at canopy | Adjust lighting distance/intensity |
| **Light Cycle** | 18h ON/6h OFF (veg), 12h ON/12h OFF (flower) | Verify daily | Adjust timer settings |
| **CO₂ Level** | 400-1000 ppm (veg), 800-1200 ppm (flower) | 1-2x daily | Adjust ventilation/CO₂ injection |
| **Air Movement** | Gentle circulation (fans on 1-4 hrs) | Visual assessment daily | Activate fans or adjust speed |

### 5.3 Plant Health Visual Assessment

**Daily Visual Assessment (by Cultivation Team Lead):**

| Assessment Area | What to Observe | Normal Signs | Deviation Signs | Action |
|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Leaf Color & Texture** | All leaves including lower canopy | Uniform green, slight texture | Yellowing, spots, wilting, curling | Document, assess nutrient status, adjust if needed |
| **Plant Structure** | Stem strength, internode spacing, branching | Strong stems, compact spacing (veg), elongated (flower) | Weak/thin stems, excessive stretching, abnormal growth | Adjust light intensity/duration or nutrients |
| **Root Condition** (visible through pot) | Root emergence from drain holes | Roots present (not excessive), healthy white color | No roots visible or excessive brown/black roots | Adjust watering or investigate root issues |
| **Pest Presence** | Visual inspection underside of leaves | No pests, clean leaves | Insects, webbing, sticky residue, holes | Implement IPM, increase monitoring frequency |
| **Disease Symptoms** | Leaf/stem/bud condition | No visible disease | Spots, mold, mildew, rust, blight | Isolate affected plant, increase air movement, reduce humidity |
| **Flower Development** (flowering phase) | Bud size, resin production, pistil color | Increasing bud density, white pistils, resin visible | Poor bud development, lack of resin, brown pistils early | Assess environmental conditions, nutrient status |

### 5.4 Monitoring Documentation

**Daily Monitoring Log Must Capture:**

- Date and time of observation
- Cultivator name and initials
- Temperature readings (high/low for day)
- Humidity readings (high/low for day)
- CO₂ level (if measured)
- Light cycle verification (on-time, off-time)
- Visual assessment summary (✓ Normal or deviation noted)
- Any corrective actions implemented
- Any deviations or concerns identified
- Signature of person conducting monitoring

## 6. CLONING & ROOTING PHASE MONITORING

### 6.1 Phase Overview

**Duration:** 7-14 days
**Objective:** Successful root development and clone establishment
**Critical Success Factor:** High humidity and proper light conditions

### 6.2 Cloning Phase-Specific Parameters

| Parameter | Target Value | Why Critical | Monitoring Frequency | Out-of-Spec Action |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|--------|--------|
| **Humidity** | 85-95% | Prevents wilting, promotes root initiation | 2x daily (morning/evening) | Adjust misting/humidifier |
| **Temperature** | 20-24°C | Optimal for rooting hormone activity | 2x daily | Adjust heat mat or cooling |
| **Light** | Low intensity, 12-16h photoperiod | Prevents stress, conserves energy for rooting | 1x daily (verify timer) | Adjust grow light distance/duration |
| **Air Movement** | Minimal (small fan on gentle) | Prevents excessive transpiration | Visual assessment 1x daily | Reduce or redirect air flow |
| **Growing Medium** | Moist but not waterlogged | Provides support and rooting hormone contact | 1-2x daily (visual check) | Adjust watering schedule |

### 6.3 Root Development Milestones

**Day 1-3:** Clone establishment
- ☐ Leaves firm (not wilting)
- ☐ Color maintained
- ☐ No visible disease

**Day 4-7:** Initial root formation
- ☐ Callus formation visible at cut site
- ☐ Possible fine root initiation
- ☐ First white root hairs emerging

**Day 8-14:** Root development
- ☐ Visible white roots (2-5 mm length)
- ☐ Root emergence from medium
- ☐ Healthy white/cream color (not brown or black)
- ☐ Clone ready for planting into vegetative containers

### 6.4 Cloning Phase Deviations

**Critical Deviation: Fungal/Mold Infection**
- **Signs:** Gray/white mold on leaves or medium, musty odor
- **Immediate Action:** Remove affected clones, increase air movement, reduce humidity to 75%, apply fungicide per label
- **Prevention:** Sterile tools, clean propagation area, proper humidity control

**Critical Deviation: Root Rot (Pythium)**
- **Signs:** Black mushy roots, foul odor, clone collapse
- **Immediate Action:** Remove affected clone, sterilize propagation tray, reduce watering frequency, increase temperature to 24°C, consider fungicide application
- **Prevention:** Sterile medium, proper moisture control, good air movement

**Major Deviation: Poor Root Development**
- **Signs:** No roots visible by Day 10-12, roots brown or mushy
- **Cause Investigation:** Growing medium pH, temperature too low, humidity too high, hormone degradation
- **Corrective Action:** Adjust environmental conditions, replace growing medium, extend propagation period if clones otherwise healthy

## 7. VEGETATIVE PHASE MONITORING

### 7.1 Phase Overview

**Duration:** 3-8 weeks depending on desired plant size
**Objective:** Establish strong root system, healthy leaf area, optimal plant structure
**Critical Success Factor:** Balanced light, nutrients, and environmental conditions

### 7.2 Vegetative Phase-Specific Parameters

| Parameter | Target Value | Notes | Action if Out of Spec |
|-----------|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Light Cycle** | 18h ON / 6h OFF | Critical for vegetative growth continuation | Adjust timer immediately |
| **Light Intensity** | 400-800 µmol/m²/s | Depends on cultivar; higher = faster growth | Adjust grow light height/intensity |
| **Temperature** | 20-26°C day, 16-20°C night | Promotes vigorous growth and nutrient uptake | Adjust HVAC/heating/cooling |
| **Humidity** | 45-60% | Prevents mold while allowing transpiration | Adjust dehumidifier/humidifier |
| **Nutrient Concentration** | EC 1.0-1.6 (soil), 1.2-1.6 (hydro) | Supports vegetative growth; avoid deficiency or toxicity | Adjust nutrient concentration, check pH |
| **pH (Growing Medium)** | 6.0-6.5 (soil), 5.5-6.5 (hydro) | Affects nutrient uptake | Adjust pH with buffers |
| **Air Movement** | Fans 4-8 hours/day | Strengthens stems, prevents pockets of stagnant air | Activate fans or adjust duration |
| **Watering** | When top 2-3 cm of medium dry | Prevents over/under watering | Assess by weight and visual inspection |

### 7.3 Vegetative Phase Plant Development

**Week 1-2: Establishment**
- ☐ New leaf emergence every 3-5 days
- ☐ Root system establishing (visible at pot edges by Week 2)
- ☐ Plant height increasing 1-2 cm per week
- ☐ Leaf color vibrant green
- ☐ No pest or disease symptoms

**Week 3-4: Rapid Growth**
- ☐ Leaf size increasing noticeably
- ☐ Internodal spacing becoming characteristic of strain
- ☐ Stem thickness increasing, becoming woody
- ☐ Plant height increasing 5-10 cm per week
- ☐ Root system filling container

**Week 5-8: Pre-Flowering Preparation**
- ☐ Plant reaching intended size for flowering (strain and cultivation plan dependent)
- ☐ Multiple branch development (if topping/training applied)
- ☐ Robust stem structure
- ☐ Full, healthy canopy
- ☐ Plants ready for transition to flowering light cycle (if flowering planned) or extended vegetative growth (for mother plants)

### 7.4 Vegetative Phase Deviations

**Critical Deviation: Nutrient Deficiency**
- **Signs:** Yellowing leaves (starting from lower canopy), smaller new leaves, stunted growth
- **Causes:** Low nutrient solution, high pH preventing nutrient uptake, root issues
- **Action:** Check EC and pH; adjust nutrient concentration if low or pH if out of range; assess root health
- **Prevention:** Regular EC and pH monitoring; maintain proper nutrient level per strain

**Critical Deviation: Nutrient Toxicity**
- **Signs:** Leaf tip burn, dark green coloration, claw-like appearance of leaf tips
- **Causes:** EC too high, accumulated salts in medium
- **Action:** Reduce nutrient concentration; monitor EC; partial water change if in hydro system
- **Prevention:** Regular EC monitoring; follow strain-specific nutrient schedule

**Major Deviation: Pest Infestation**
- **Signs:** Spider mites (webbing, stippled leaves), whitefly (small white insects), aphids, scale
- **Action:** Increase air movement; implement IPM (neem oil, insecticidal soap); quarantine affected plants; increase monitoring frequency
- **Prevention:** Regular visual inspections; IPM as preventive measure; clean cultivation area; pest monitoring traps

**Major Deviation: Fungal Disease**
- **Signs:** Powdery mildew (white powder on leaves), botrytis (gray mold on stems/buds, later phase), rust (yellow-brown spots)
- **Action:** Increase air movement; reduce humidity; remove affected leaves; apply fungicide if needed
- **Prevention:** Proper humidity control; good air circulation; avoid overhead watering; remove dead leaves regularly

**Moderate Deviation: Light Burn**
- **Signs:** Bleached leaf appearance, leaf curling upward, brown patches (especially at canopy top)
- **Causes:** Grow lights too close to canopy
- **Action:** Raise lights to appropriate distance for strain; monitor plant recovery
- **Prevention:** Maintain appropriate light distance; monitor plant height and adjust light height accordingly

## 8. FLOWERING PHASE MONITORING

### 8.1 Phase Overview

**Duration:** 8-12 weeks depending on strain and maturity desired
**Objective:** Optimal flower development, cannabinoid and terpene production, disease prevention
**Critical Success Factor:** Precise light cycle maintenance, appropriate nutrient balance, strict environmental control

### 8.2 Flowering Phase-Specific Parameters

| Parameter | Week 1-3 | Week 4-6 | Week 7-10 | Week 11-12 (Final Ripening) |
|-----------|----------|----------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Light Cycle** | 12h ON / 12h OFF (strictly maintained) | 12h ON / 12h OFF | 12h ON / 12h OFF | 12h ON / 12h OFF (critical - any light cycle deviation triggers re-vegging) |
| **Temperature** | 20-24°C day, 16-20°C night | 20-23°C day, 16-20°C night | 18-22°C day, 14-18°C night | 15-20°C day, 12-16°C night (cooler for color/terpene development) |
| **Humidity** | 45-55% | 40-50% (reduce to prevent mold) | 35-45% (strictly enforce) | 30-40% (critical to prevent bud rot/botrytis) |
| **EC (Nutrients)** | 1.2-1.6 | 1.4-1.8 | 1.6-2.0 | 0.8-1.2 (reduce for ripening) |
| **Light Intensity** | 600-900 µmol/m²/s | 700-1000 µmol/m²/s | 700-1000 µmol/m²/s | 600-900 µmol/m²/s |
| **Air Movement** | 6-8 hours fan/day | 8-10 hours fan/day | 10-12 hours fan/day | 12+ hours (maximum) |

### 8.3 Flowering Stage Development

**Week 1-3: Flower Initiation**
- ☐ Pistil emergence (white hairs) at nodes
- ☐ Flower sites forming
- ☐ Plant height stabilizing (pre-flowering stretch complete)
- ☐ Nutrient demand increasing
- ☐ Appearance: Early flower buds visible, small and light green

**Week 4-6: Bud Development**
- ☐ Bud size increasing noticeably
- ☐ Sugar leaf coverage of buds
- ☐ Resin production beginning (visible trichomes)
- ☐ Plant requiring full nutrient support
- ☐ Appearance: Medium-sized buds, denser structure, resin starting to coat surfaces

**Week 7-10: Bud Maturation**
- ☐ Bud size and density at maximum
- ☐ Resin production peak
- ☐ Pistil color changing (white → amber/brown)
- ☐ Trichome maturity approaching (clear → cloudy → amber)
- ☐ Nutrient uptake peak at weeks 7-8, then declining
- ☐ Appearance: Large, dense buds, heavy resin coverage, rich aroma developing

**Week 11-12: Final Ripening**
- ☐ Bud structure complete and firm
- ☐ Pistils majority amber/brown (if genetic expression normal)
- ☐ Trichomes nearing desired maturity
- ☐ Nutrient levels declining (flushing period if applicable)
- ☐ Final cannabinoid and terpene profile developing
- ☐ Plant approaching harvest readiness

### 8.4 Trichome Maturity Assessment (Non-IPC Routine Monitoring)

**NOTE:** Formal IPC (In-Process Control) trichome maturity determination is covered in separate SOP. This section covers routine plant health monitoring perspective.

**Trichome Development Stages (Visual Assessment):**

| Stage | Appearance | Plant Cannabinoid Status | Notes |
|-------|-----------|--------------------------|-------|
| **Clear** | Transparent, glass-like | THCA abundant, THC minimal | Too early for harvest (immature cannabinoid profile) |
| **Cloudy/Milky** | Opaque, white/milky appearance | THCA converting to THC | Approaching optimal harvest timing |
| **Amber** | Golden brown color | THCA mostly converted to THC, some CBN formation | Full maturity, continued ripening possible |
| **All Amber (Brown)** | Entirely dark brown | Significant THC degradation to CBN | Over-mature for most commercial products |

**Optimal Harvest Window (Typical):**
- 70% Cloudy + 30% Amber = Peak THC with balanced effect profile
- 50% Cloudy + 50% Amber = Mature, slightly heavier effect (more CBN)
- Strain variation significant; some cultivars optimal at earlier or later stages

**Monitoring During Flowering:**
- ☐ Week 1-7: General plant health (no formal trichome assessment needed)
- ☐ Week 8-9: Begin routine visual trichome observation (1-2x per week)
- ☐ Week 9-12: Weekly or more frequent trichome assessment
- ☐ Week 11-12: Determine harvest readiness based on trichome maturity + plant condition

### 8.5 Critical Flowering Phase Deviations

**CRITICAL: Early Flowering or Re-Vegging**
- **Cause:** Light cycle interruption (unexpected light during dark period)
- **Prevention:** Strict light cycle control; verify timer daily; check for light leaks from other sources
- **Action:** Restore proper light cycle immediately; plant may recover or may produce abnormal flowers

**CRITICAL: Botrytis (Gray Mold) - Budrot**
- **Signs:** Gray fuzz on buds/stems, musty smell, rapid deterioration during final weeks
- **Risk:** Highest in final 3-4 weeks when buds dense and humidity high
- **Prevention:**
  - Humidity <40% in weeks 10-12 (strictly enforced)
  - Maximum air movement (fans 12+ hours/day)
  - Remove dead leaves and lower plant material to improve air flow
  - Space plants for maximum canopy air penetration
  - Scout daily for early signs
- **Action if Detected:**
  - Remove affected bud and surrounding area
  - Increase air movement to maximum
  - Reduce humidity further if possible
  - Increase monitoring frequency to daily
  - Do not apply fungicides (not suitable for final weeks)

**CRITICAL: Hermaphroditism (Female Plant Producing Male Flowers)**
- **Signs:** Male pollen sacs developing among female flowers; indicates stress response
- **Cause:** Genetic predisposition triggered by stress (light cycle interruption, temperature stress, nutrient deficiency, overcrowding)
- **Risk:** Seed production in batch, product specification non-compliance (Ph.Eur. seedless requirement)
- **Prevention:**
  - Genetic strain quality control
  - Proper environmental conditions (avoid stress)
  - Correct light cycle (no interruptions)
  - Adequate spacing and air flow
  - Proper nutrient management
- **Action if Detected:**
  - Remove hermaphroditic plant(s) immediately to prevent pollination
  - Increase monitoring of remaining plants for pollen sacs
  - Monitor female plants for developing seeds as harvest approaches
  - Adjust environmental conditions to reduce continued stress

**MAJOR: Potassium Deficiency (Late-Stage)**
- **Signs:** Scorching leaf edges, early leaf drop, weak bud structure
- **Cause:** High EC pushing out potassium, insufficient potassium in nutrient formula
- **Prevention:** Monitor EC; ensure potassium adequate in nutrient formulation for flowering
- **Action:** Reduce EC slightly; increase potassium concentration; assess nutrient uptake

**MAJOR: Magnesium Deficiency**
- **Signs:** Yellowing between veins (interveinal chlorosis) in older leaves while veins remain green
- **Cause:** EC too high (antagonizes magnesium uptake), pH out of range
- **Prevention:** Monitor EC and pH; maintain proper ratios
- **Action:** Reduce EC if elevated; adjust pH; may apply magnesium sulfate (Epsom salt) foliar spray

## 9. HARVEST PHASE MONITORING

### 9.1 Harvest Timing Determination

**Harvest Readiness Assessment:**

**Visual Indicators:**
- ☐ Trichome maturity at target level (see Flowering Phase trichome assessment)
- ☐ Pistil color at target stage (typically 70-100% amber/brown)
- ☐ Bud structure firm and dense
- ☐ Overall plant appearance healthy (no disease, pest damage, or severe nutrient issues)
- ☐ No visible mold or rot in buds

**Strain-Specific Targets:**
- Documented harvest window for cultivar being grown (typically days 55-70 of flowering, varies by strain)
- Historical records from previous batches of same strain
- Target cannabinoid profile if formal post-harvest testing planned

**Batch Record Completion:**
- ☐ All monitoring data entered
- ☐ Critical deviations documented and addressed
- ☐ Trichome maturity assessment recorded
- ☐ Cultivation Manager approval obtained
- ☐ Batch ready for harvesting

### 9.2 Harvest Procedure Overview

**Pre-Harvest Checklist:**
- ☐ Harvest date confirmed based on trichome maturity and plant health
- ☐ Harvest area prepared (clean, organized, tools sterilized)
- ☐ Batch ID labels prepared and ready
- ☐ Harvest containers prepared (clean, GMP-suitable materials)
- ☐ Harvest personnel assigned and briefed
- ☐ Environmental conditions suitable for harvest (humidity 45-55% optimal; temperature 15-20°C optimal)

**Harvest Documentation:**
- ☐ Harvest date and time recorded
- ☐ Personnel names and initials
- ☐ Plants harvested verified (batch plants accounted for)
- ☐ Estimated yield noted
- ☐ Visual quality assessment (appearance, visible defects)
- ☐ Facility conditions during harvest

## 10. MOTHER PLANT MONITORING

### 10.1 Mother Plant Purpose and Management

**Purpose:** Propagation source for all clones in cultivation cycle

**Characteristics:**
- Maintained indefinitely in vegetative state
- Typically replaced every 6 months (or per cultivation plan)
- May consist of multiple strains for genetic diversity
- Location: Dedicated mother plant area with separate environmental controls

### 10.2 Mother Plant-Specific Monitoring

**Monitoring Frequency:** Daily (same as vegetative plants)

**Special Considerations:**

| Aspect | Requirement | Reason |
|--------|------------|--------|
| **Light Cycle** | 18h ON / 6h OFF (strictly maintained) | Prevents flowering induction |
| **Clone Take Rate** | >80% successful rooting | Indicates mother plant health and vigor |
| **Disease Status** | Zero tolerance for disease in mother plants | Disease propagates to all daughter clones |
| **Pest Status** | Zero tolerance for pest infestation | Pests propagate to all clones |
| **Genetic Drift** | Monitor for atypical growth characteristics | Indicates potential genetic mutation or environmental stress |
| **Nutrient Status** | Sustained vigor and leaf color | Clones inherit genetic vigor from healthy mothers |
| **Replacement Timing** | Every 6 months per plan (or when vigor declines) | Prevents accumulation of genetic drift or disease |

### 10.3 Mother Plant Health Milestones

**Month 1-2: Establishment**
- ☐ Mother plant growing vigorously
- ☐ Clone take rate >80%
- ☐ Leaf color vibrant green
- ☐ Pest/disease free

**Month 3-4: Peak Production**
- ☐ Maximum vegetative vigor
- ☐ Clone take rate >85-90%
- ☐ Consistent clone quality
- ☐ Continued pest/disease free status

**Month 5-6: Late Production**
- ☐ Slight decline in clone take rate (may drop to 75-80%)
- ☐ Mother plant possibly showing minor nutrient stress
- ☐ Age-related vigor decline possible
- ☐ Plan replacement timing

**Month 6+: Retirement**
- ☐ Clone take rate declining below acceptable threshold
- ☐ Mother plant removed from production
- ☐ Replacement mother plant established from available clones or new genetics
- ☐ Cycle restarts with new mother plant

## 11. SELECTION/R&D PLANT MONITORING

### 11.1 Selection/R&D Purpose

**Purpose:** Evaluate new strains/phenotypes for commercial viability

**Typical Structure:**
- Multiple strains under evaluation (3-10 strains common)
- Each strain may include 5-10+ individual plants (different phenotypes)
- Grown in parallel to identify best-performing individuals

### 11.2 Selection Plant Monitoring Approach

**Option 1: Individual Plant Records**
- ☐ Each plant assigned individual ID (e.g., Strain A-Plant 1, Strain A-Plant 2, etc.)
- ☐ Separate monitoring log for each plant
- ☐ Detailed observations of individual growth characteristics
- ☐ Individual trichome maturity assessment
- ☐ Individual final evaluation and selection decision

**Option 2: Strain-Level Records**
- ☐ Strain assigned ID (e.g., Strain A)
- ☐ Single monitoring log for all plants of strain
- ☐ Plants evaluated as group initially
- ☐ Individual plants selected for detailed assessment if promising
- ☐ Final selection decision at strain and/or individual phenotype level

**Recommendation:** Use Option 1 (Individual Plant Records) for detailed phenotype selection; use Option 2 (Strain Level) if preliminary screening only

### 11.3 Selection Plant Monitoring Parameters

**Standard Cultivation Monitoring:**
- Follow all guidelines in Sections 6-8 (Cloning → Vegetative → Flowering)
- Same environmental conditions as commercial plants
- Same nutrient and water management

**Additional Selection-Specific Parameters:**

| Parameter | What to Monitor | Documentation |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Growth Rate** | Plant height at each week, internode spacing | Weekly measurements, photos |
| **Plant Structure** | Branching pattern, stem diameter, overall shape | Visual assessment, photos |
| **Leaf Characteristics** | Leaf size, shape, color, texture, serration pattern | Detailed descriptions, photos |
| **Pest/Disease Resistance** | Susceptibility to common pests/diseases compared to other strains | Qualitative assessment relative to other strains |
| **Cannabinoid Profile** | Expected THC/CBD ratio based on strain genetics, visual indicators | Post-harvest testing (if applicable) |
| **Aroma/Terpene Profile** | Distinctive smell, volatile characteristics | Sensory assessment, notes during flowering |
| **Flower Characteristics** | Bud density, resin production, pistil color, apparent yield | Photos, qualitative assessment |
| **Harvest Timing** | Trichome maturity achievement relative to expected timing | Monitoring records during final weeks |
| **Post-Harvest Characteristics** | Yield (wet/dry weight), cure characteristics, final appearance | Measurements and observations post-harvest |

### 11.4 Selection Decision Documentation

**Evaluation Criteria (for each plant/strain):**

- ☐ **Growth Performance:** Expected for genetics? Better/worse than other strains?
- ☐ **Environmental Stress Response:** How plant responds to slight environmental fluctuations
- ☐ **Pest/Disease Susceptibility:** Natural resistance observed?
- ☐ **Cannabinoid Profile:** Achieves target potency? Consistent with genetics?
- ☐ **Commercial Viability:** Yield potential? Cultivation ease? Market appeal?
- ☐ **Overall Assessment:** Recommend for continued cultivation/commercialization?

**Selection Outcome:**
- ☐ **Approved for Commercial Use:** Plant/strain meets criteria, proceed to production scale
- ☐ **Approved with Monitoring:** Promising but requires continued observation, plan second evaluation
- ☐ **Rejected:** Does not meet criteria, discontinue cultivation
- ☐ **Approved but On Standby:** Viable backup if primary strain has issues

## 12. DEVIATION MANAGEMENT

### 12.1 Deviation Definition

A **Deviation** occurs when any monitoring observation or measurement falls outside the defined acceptable range for that phase and parameter.

**Examples:**
- Temperature exceeds 28°C (above target 18-26°C)
- Humidity drops below 35% (below target 40-45% minimum)
- Light cycle interrupted by unplanned light exposure
- Pest insects visible during inspection
- Visible disease symptoms (mold, rust, spots)
- Plant shows severe nutrient deficiency symptoms
- Environmental monitoring equipment malfunction (reading unavailable)

### 12.2 Deviation Response Protocol

**Immediate Response (Within 2 Hours of Detection):**

1. ☐ **Stop and Assess:** Halt current activity if necessary
2. ☐ **Document Finding:** Record deviation details (time, observation, measurement, location)
3. ☐ **Notify Supervisor:** Team Lead notifies Cultivation Manager immediately
4. ☐ **Implement Containment:** If plant health at risk (pests, disease), isolate affected area
5. ☐ **Corrective Action:** Implement immediate correction:
   - Temperature out of range? Adjust HVAC immediately
   - Pest detected? Increase monitoring frequency, apply IPM
   - Disease detected? Increase air movement, reduce humidity, consider removal
   - Light leak? Seal immediately
   - Equipment malfunction? Troubleshoot or replace equipment

**Deviation Documentation (Within 24 Hours):**
- Complete deviation entry on Plant Health Monitoring form
- Describe deviation in detail
- State corrective action taken
- Signature of Team Lead and Cultivation Manager
- Note follow-up monitoring planned

### 12.3 Critical vs. Major vs. Minor Deviations

**CRITICAL Deviations (Immediate Escalation Required):**
- Light cycle interruption (lights on during dark period)
- Pest infestation (spider mites, aphids, whitefly, fungus gnats)
- Disease detection (Botrytis, powdery mildew, rust, root rot)
- Temperature exceeding ±5°C from target (e.g., >31°C or <11°C)
- Humidity exceeding ±15% from target (e.g., >75% or <25%)
- Hermaphroditism detected (female plants developing pollen sacs)

→ **Action:** Immediate notification to Cultivation Manager; corrective action within 2 hours; continued monitoring; possible CAPA if systemic issue

**MAJOR Deviations (Same-Shift Correction Required):**
- Temperature off target by 2-5°C (e.g., 15-17°C or 27-28°C)
- Humidity off target by 10-15% (e.g., 30-35% or 60-70%)
- Visible nutrient deficiency symptoms
- Leaf spot or early disease symptoms
- Equipment reading unavailable (sensor malfunction)
- Watering over/underwatered (visual assessment)

→ **Action:** Corrective action within 4-8 hours; increased monitoring; deviation documented; may require CAPA if recurring

**MINOR Deviations (Within-Day Correction Acceptable):**
- Temperature off target by 1-2°C (e.g., 18-19°C or 25-26°C)
- Humidity off target by 5-10% (e.g., 35-40% or 55-60%)
- Slight leaf color changes (minor nutrient stress)
- Single fallen leaf or minor branch break
- Minor equipment calibration drift
- Watering slightly delayed from schedule

→ **Action:** Corrective action within 24 hours; standard documentation; routine monitoring continues

## 13. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS

### 13.1 Corrective Action Development

When a deviation or plant health issue is identified, the following actions are determined:

**Root Cause Identification:**
- Why did the deviation occur?
- Was it environmental (equipment failure, setting error)?
- Was it procedural (staff error, procedure violation)?
- Was it biological (plant genetics, pest/disease)?
- Was it systemic (inadequate maintenance, training gap)?

**Corrective Action Planning:**
- What specific action will eliminate the root cause?
- Who is responsible for implementation?
- What is the timeline for completion?
- How will effectiveness be verified?
- Document in CAPA system if systemic issue identified

**Examples:**

| Deviation | Root Cause | Corrective Action | Timeline |
|-----------|-----------|------------------|----------|
| Temperature exceeded 28°C during day | HVAC thermostat drift | Calibrate thermostats, verify HVAC operating properly | Within 24 hrs |
| Humidity dropped to 35% | Dehumidifier running continuously | Adjust dehumidifier setting, check for equipment issue | Within 4 hrs |
| Spider mites detected | Insufficient air movement allowing pest proliferation | Increase fan operation to 12 hrs/day; apply neem oil IPM | Within 2 hrs |
| Powdery mildew on leaves | High humidity + poor air circulation | Reduce humidity to <45%; increase air movement to 10+ hrs/day; remove affected leaves | Within 4 hrs |
| Nutrient deficiency symptoms | EC too low in nutrient solution | Increase EC to target range (1.2-1.6); monitor leaf color recovery | Within 8 hrs |

### 13.2 Effectiveness Verification

**Corrective Action Effectiveness Check:**
- Implemented within specified timeline? ✓
- Root cause actually addressed? ✓
- Parameter returned to target range? ✓
- Plant health status improved? ✓
- No adverse effects from corrective action? ✓

**Follow-Up Monitoring:**
- Enhanced monitoring frequency for 1-2 weeks after corrective action
- Document improvement in plant health observations
- If deviation recurs → escalate to CAPA for systemic investigation

- ☐ Daily monitoring procedures and documentation requirements
- ☐ Environmental monitoring equipment operation and reading

- ☐ Common pest and disease identification
- ☐ Deviation recognition and reporting
